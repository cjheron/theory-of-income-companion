Aggregation & the Social Planner: Cheatsheet

Planner weights, the representative agent, the welfare theorems via first-order conditions, Gorman aggregation and its limits. Lessons 21–25 · Slides · Glossary · Index

Utility possibility set L21 · slides 3–4

  • \(U=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^I:\ u_i\le u^i(x^i)\ \forall i\ \text{for some feasible}\ \{x^i,y^j\}\}\).
  • It is downward comprehensive by construction (the "\(\le\)"): \(u\in U,\ u'\le u\Rightarrow u'\in U\).
  • PO allocation ⇒ \(\bar u=(u^i(\bar x^i))_i\) is on the (upper) boundary of \(U\).
  • \(X^i\) and \(Y\) convex ⇒ the set of feasible allocations is convex (the constraint is linear).
  • Add CC (every \(u^i\) concave) ⇒ \(U\) convex: \(u^i(tx^i+(1-t)x'^i)\ge tu^i(x^i)+(1-t)u^i(x'^i)\).
  • Concavity is cardinal (\(e^{u}\) can destroy it). Quasi-concavity is ordinal, and it is not enough: one good, \(u^i=(x^i)^2\), \(\bar e=1\) gives the frontier \(\sqrt{u_1}+\sqrt{u_2}=1\), which bows inward.

Planner-weight theorem L21 · slides 5–10

Assume \(u^i\) strictly increasing, HH, CC, FF, and \(\operatorname{Int}U\ne\emptyset\). Let \(W(\lambda)\): \(\max_{\{x^i,y^j\}}\sum_i\lambda_iu^i(x^i)\) s.t. feasibility.

  1. Sufficiency. \(\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^I_{++}\) and \(\{\bar x,\bar y\}\) solves \(W(\lambda)\) ⇒ PO.
  2. Necessity. \(\{\bar x,\bar y\}\) PO ⇒ it solves \(W(\lambda)\) for some \(\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^I_+\), \(\lambda\ne0\).

Sufficiency. A Pareto improvement \(\hat x\) has \(u^i(\hat x^i)\ge u^i(\bar x^i)\) for all \(i\), strictly for some. With every \(\lambda_i>0\), \(\sum\lambda_iu^i(\hat x^i)>\sum\lambda_iu^i(\bar x^i)\). This contradicts optimality. No convexity is used.

Necessity. \(U\) is convex and \(\bar u\in\partial U\), so a supporting hyperplane exists: \(\lambda^*\ne0\) with \(\lambda^*\bar u\ge\lambda^*u\) for all \(u\in U\). Sign: if \(\lambda^*_i\lt 0\), then \(\bar u-\varepsilon e_i\in U\) (comprehensive) and \(\lambda^*(\bar u-\varepsilon e_i)>\lambda^*\bar u\). Contradiction, so \(\lambda^*\ge0\).

Zero weights

A PO point may need some \(\lambda_i=0\) (e.g. at a corner of the frontier). Then \(W(\lambda)\) can have other maximizers that are not PO: they give agent \(i\) less without changing anyone else's utility. "Solves \(W\) for some \(\lambda\ge0\)" ⇏ PO. Picture: \(\lambda\) is the normal to a supporting line of \(U\) at \(\bar u\); if \(U\) is non-convex, inward-bowed frontier points are PO but are supported by no \(\lambda\).

Representative agent L22 · slides 11–14

  • \(u(x;\lambda)=\max_{\{x^i\in X^i\}}\sum_i\lambda_iu^i(x^i)\) s.t. \(\sum_ix^i=x\), defined on \(\sum_iX^i\times\mathbb{R}^I_+\). It involves no technology, so the production side can be handled by one "Crusoe" facing \(Y\).
  • Concave in \(x\) if every \(X^i\) is convex and every \(u^i\) concave: mix the two optimal allocations, which is feasible at the mixed aggregate. It is HD1 in \(\lambda\).
  • CE ⇒ PO (1WT) ⇒ some \(\lambda\) whose single agent reproduces the CE. PO ⇒ CE for some ownership (2WT) ⇒ the same.
  • Exchange economy: \(Y=-\mathbb{R}^m_+\) (free disposal only); feasibility is \(\sum_ix^i\le\sum_ie^i\equiv\bar e\).
  • Envelope theorem: \(\partial u(\bar e;\lambda)/\partial x_l=\gamma_l\), the shadow value of good \(l\).

Welfare theorems via FOCs: Negishi weights L23 · slides 20–25

$$\lambda_i=\frac{\kappa}{\mu_i},\qquad \gamma_l=\kappa\,p_l,\qquad \kappa>0\ \text{arbitrary (normalization).}$$

1WT (CE ⇒ PO). Use necessity of the household FOC at the CE. Divide by \(\mu_i\): \(\frac1{\mu_i}u^i_l=p_l\), which is the planner FOC with \(\lambda_i=1/\mu_i\), \(\gamma=p\). Then use sufficiency of the planner FOC, plus feasibility, ⇒ solves \(W\) ⇒ PO.

2WT (PO ⇒ CE). Use necessity of the planner FOC at \(\bar x\) with \(\lambda\gg0\). Set \(p=\gamma\), \(\mu_i=1/\lambda_i\), and \(e^i=\bar x^i\) (so \(\bar x^i\) is affordable). Then use sufficiency of the household FOC ⇒ \(\bar x^i\) is optimal; it is feasible ⇒ CE.

  • Distribution: rich agents consume a lot ⇒ low \(\mu_i\) (concavity) ⇒ high \(\lambda_i\). Goods with high \(p_l\) have high social value \(\gamma_l\).
  • Prices are a weighted average of marginal utilities with good-independent weights: \(p_l\propto\sum_i\frac1{\mu_i}u^i_l(\bar x^i)\) (Fall 2025 MT1 T/F: True, needs interiority).
  • In general \(p\) and \(\bar x\) depend on the endowment distribution \(\{e^i\}\), and \(\gamma\) and \(\bar x\) depend on \(\lambda\).

Gorman aggregation L24 · slides 26–30

Assume \(u^i(x)=g^i(h(x))\), with \(g^i\) strictly increasing and concave and \(h\) common and HD1. Endowments are proportional, \(e^i=\delta^i\bar e\) with \(\sum_i\delta^i=1\). Then \(\bar x^i=\delta^i\bar e\) (no trade), and

$$p_l=\kappa\,\frac{\partial h(\bar e)}{\partial x_l},\qquad \mu_i=\frac{g^{i\prime}\big(h(\delta^i\bar e)\big)}{\kappa},\qquad \lambda_i=\frac{\kappa}{g^{i\prime}\big(h(\delta^i\bar e)\big)},\qquad \gamma_l=\kappa\,\frac{\partial h(\bar e)}{\partial x_l}.$$

  • Key step: \(h\) HD1 ⇒ \(h_l\) HD0 ⇒ \(h_l(\delta^i\bar e)=h_l(\bar e)\). The household FOC \(g^{i\prime}h_l(\delta^i\bar e)=\mu_ip_l\) then holds with the \(\mu_i\) above.
  • Relative prices are independent of \(\{\delta^i\}\), and \(\gamma_l/\gamma_k\) is independent of \(\lambda\). The level of \(\gamma\) depends on the normalization; \(\lambda\) depends on \(\delta\).
  • With common \(g\): larger \(\delta^i\) ⇒ larger \(\lambda_i\) (\(g'\) decreasing, \(h\) increasing). With different \(g^i\), this need not hold.
  • Arbitrary endowments: all that is needed is \(p\cdot e^i=\delta^i\,p\cdot\bar e\), i.e. define \(\delta^i=p\cdot e^i/p\cdot\bar e\) at \(p\propto\nabla h(\bar e)\). Then \(\bar x^i=\delta^i\bar e\), and there is trade.

Which preference families aggregate? L25 · slides 31–37

Method: planner FOC \(\lambda_iu^i_l=\gamma_l\) → solve for \(x^i_l\) → sum using \(\sum_ix^i_l=\bar e_l\) → solve for \(\gamma_l(\lambda)\) → test whether \(\gamma_l/\gamma_k\) depends on \(\lambda\). Notation: \(\bar\theta=\sum_i\theta^i\).

Unifying idea

Every family marked "Yes" is \(u^i=g\big(H(x-\theta^i)\big)\), with \(g\) and HD1 \(H\) common across agents: identical homothetic preferences after an agent-specific shift of the origin (slide 37). Let \(\hat x^i=x^i-\theta^i\), so feasibility becomes \(\sum_i\hat x^i=\bar e-\bar\theta\), and \(\gamma\propto\nabla H(\bar e-\bar\theta)\). Equivalently, Engel curves are linear with a common slope (Gorman polar form).

Representative agent with translations (Fall 2025 Final Q2) L25

  • Problem: \(U(\bar e;\lambda)=\max\sum_i\lambda_iH(c^i-\theta^i)\) s.t. \(\sum_ic^i=\bar e\), with \(H\) HD1, increasing and quasi-concave (hence concave and superadditive on its positive cone).
  • Equal weights (the CE weights, since \(\mu_i=1/b(p)\) is common under HD1 \(H\)): \(U(\bar e;\lambda)=\lambda\,H(\bar e-\bar\theta)\).
  • Unequal weights with \(c^i\ge\theta^i\): \(U=(\max_i\lambda_i)\,H(\bar e-\bar\theta)\), with all the surplus going to the top-weight agent. In every case \(\nabla_{\bar e}U\propto\nabla H(\bar e-\bar\theta)\), independent of \(\lambda\).
Circulating answer is wrong

\(U(\bar e;\lambda)=H\big(\bar e-\sum_i\lambda_i\theta^i\big)\) fails two checks. With \(I=1\) it should be \(\lambda_1H(\bar e-\theta^1)\). And any such value function is HD1 in \(\lambda\), which this formula is not. The right transformation is the sum \(\bar\theta=\sum_i\theta^i\), not a \(\lambda\)-weighted sum.
