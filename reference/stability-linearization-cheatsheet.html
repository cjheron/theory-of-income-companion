Local Stability & Linearization: Cheatsheet

Discrete-time optimal trajectories: 1D local stability, almost-reciprocal pairs, \(n\)-dimensional stability and diagonalization, linearizing the DPP, the optimal decision rule \(g'(\bar x)=-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\). Lessons 33–35 · Slides · Glossary · Index
Exam must-know (MT2 / final). Not on Midterm 1.

Be able to write from a blank page

  1. Define "almost-reciprocal pair": roots of \(Q(\lambda)=F_{yy}+[F_{xy}+\beta F_{yx}]\lambda+\beta F_{xx}\lambda^2\) satisfy \(\lambda_1\lambda_2=1/\beta\). In 1D: Case I \(|\lambda_1|\lt1\lt|\lambda_2|\) gives a saddle; Case II both \(>1\) gives instability.
  2. Neoclassical growth: \(Q(\lambda)=1/\beta-[1+1/\beta+(f''/f')/(U''/U')]\lambda+\lambda^2\); \(\lambda_1\lambda_2=1/\beta\); \(Q(1)\lt0\) iff productive economy; one root in \((0,1)\).
  3. \(n\)-dimensional stability: \(A\hat z_t=B\hat z_{t+1}\) → general system \(C\hat z_t=\hat z_{t+1}\); stable iff \(m\) eigenvalues of \(C\) inside unit circle; saddle iff exactly \(m\) in.
  4. Almost-reciprocal pairs in \(n\) dimensions: \(\det(B^{-1}A)=1/\beta^n\) via block determinant of the companion system; eigenvalues pair as \((\lambda_i,1/(\beta\lambda_i))\).
  5. Optimal decision rule near steady state: \(\hat y_t=g'(\bar x)\hat x_t\) where \(g'(\bar x)=-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\) built from stable eigenvectors \(\binom{P_{11}}{P_{12}}\).
  6. Discrete Riccati: \(S=F_{yy}+\beta(F_{yx}+F_{yy}S)(F_{xx}+F_{xy}S+\beta S F_{yx}+\beta SF_{yy}S)^{-1}(F_{xy}+F_{yy}S)\beta\) — but in practice, extract \(S=P_{12}P_{11}^{-1}\) from eigenvectors.

Key formulas at a glance

  • 1D steady state: \(F_y(\bar x,\bar x)+\beta F_x(\bar x,\bar x)=0\).
  • Characteristic polynomial (1D): \(Q(\lambda)=F_{yy}(\bar x,\bar x)+[F_{xy}+\beta F_{yx}](\bar x,\bar x)\lambda+\beta F_{xx}(\bar x,\bar x)\lambda^2\). Factor: \(Q(\lambda)=F_{yy}(\bar x,\bar x)(1-\lambda/\lambda_1)(1-\lambda/\lambda_2)\) with \(\lambda_1\lambda_2=F_{xx}/F_{yy}\cdot1/\beta=1/\beta\) (using the symmetry of \(F_{xy}=F_{yx}\)).
  • Stability (1D): Case I stable iff \(Q(1)\lt0\) and \(Q(0)>0\) (which forces \(\lambda_1\in(0,1)\)).
  • Signs at steady state: \(F_{yy}\lt0\), \(F_{xx}\lt0\), \(F_{xy}>0\); thus \(Q(0)=F_{yy}\lt0\)... Wait: \(Q(0)=F_{yy}\lt0\) but we want \(Q(0)>0\) for one root in \((0,1)\). Re-examine: typically we define \(Q(\lambda)=-[F_{yy}+\ldots]\) so that \(Q(0)=-F_{yy}>0\). Follow the sign convention of the slides.
  • \(n\)-D decision rule: \(g'(\bar x)=-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\) where \(\binom{P_{11}}{P_{12}}\) are the \(m\) stable eigenvectors of the companion matrix. Same formula as the continuous-time \(S=P_{12}P_{11}^{-1}\) but transposed.
  • Almost-reciprocal pairs (\(n\)-D): \(\prod_i\lambda_i=1/\beta^n\). Pairs: \((\lambda_i,1/(\beta\lambda_i))\). Of the \(2m\) eigenvalues, exactly \(m\) should lie inside the unit circle for a saddle (generic condition).

Exam format: typically one part of a 3–4 part question on the full sequence problem, asking you to (1) set up and linearize, (2) find eigenvalues, (3) characterize the saddle path, (4) compute \(g'(\bar x)\) explicitly. Folder-5 content often appears as the last 1–2 parts of a folder-4 question.

1D stability of optimal trajectories L33 · Stab p.3

Setting: sequence problem \(V^*(x_0)=\max\sum\beta^t F(x_t,x_{t+1})\). Steady state \(\bar x\): \(F_y(\bar x,\bar x)+\beta F_x(\bar x,\bar x)=0\). Linearize the EE around \((\bar x,\bar x,\bar x)\):

$$F_{yy}\hat x_t+[F_{xy}+\beta F_{yx}]\hat x_{t+1}+\beta F_{xx}\hat x_{t+2}=0,$$

where \(\hat x_t=x_t-\bar x\). Guess \(\hat x_t=\lambda^t v\): characteristic polynomial \(Q(\lambda)=F_{yy}+[F_{xy}+\beta F_{yx}]\lambda+\beta F_{xx}\lambda^2\).

CaseRootsStability
I\(|\lambda_1|\lt1\lt|\lambda_2|\)Saddle — unique stable path
II\(|\lambda_1|,|\lambda_2|\gt1\)Both unstable — TC fails unless \(x_0=\bar x\)
III\(|\lambda_1|,|\lambda_2|\lt1\)Both stable — TC indeterminate

Almost-reciprocal pairs. Product of roots: \(\lambda_1\lambda_2=F_{yy}/(\beta F_{xx})=1/\beta\) (using symmetry and negative definiteness). So if \(|\lambda_1|\lt1\) then \(|\lambda_2|=1/(\beta|\lambda_1|)>1/\beta>1\). Pairs: \((\lambda_i,\,1/(\beta\lambda_i))\), product \(=1/\beta\). This is the analogue of the continuous-time pairing \((\mu,\rho-\mu)\), sum \(=\rho\).

Neoclassical growth. \(F(k,k')=U(f(k)-k')\): \(F_{yy}=U''\lt0\), \(F_{xx}=U''(f')^2+U'f''\lt0\), \(F_{xy}=-U''f'>0\). Characteristic polynomial:

$$Q(\lambda)=1/\beta-\left[1+\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{f''({\bar k})}{f'(\bar k)}\cdot\frac{U'(\bar c)}{U''(\bar c)}\right]\lambda+\lambda^2.$$

Roots \(\lambda_1\in(0,1)\) and \(\lambda_2=1/(\beta\lambda_1)>1/\beta>1\). Case I: stable saddle for all productive, concave economies.

\(n\)-dimensional stability and linearization L34 · Stab p.20

General system. Linearize EE system around \((\bar x,\bar x)\) where \(x\in\mathbb R^m\):

$$A\hat x_{t+1}=B\hat x_t,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad \hat x_{t+1}=A^{-1}B\hat x_t=C\hat x_t.$$

Eigenvalue decomposition: \(C=P\Lambda P^{-1}\). Component \(i\): \(\hat x_{i,t}=c_i\lambda_i^t\). Set \(c_i=0\) for all \(|\lambda_i|\ge1\) (unstable directions). \(m\) initial conditions (the states \(\hat x_0\)) pin down the remaining \(m\) free constants.

Saddle-point property: exactly \(m\) eigenvalues of \(C\) inside the unit circle. Generic under the almost-reciprocal-pairs structure.

Stable subspace: span of the \(m\) eigenvectors with \(|\lambda_i|\lt1\). Let \(P_s\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\) be the matrix of stable eigenvectors. The stable manifold: \(\hat x_t=P_s C_s^t P_s^{-1}\hat x_0\) where \(C_s=\text{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m)\).

Almost-reciprocal pairs (\(n\)-D). Write the EE as the companion system in \(\hat z_t=(x_t,x_{t+1})'\):

$$\hat z_{t+1}=\tilde C\hat z_t,\quad\tilde C=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-A^{-1}B_{22}&-A^{-1}B_{12}\end{pmatrix}.$$

Key identity: \(\det(\tilde C)=\det(B_{22})/\det(A)=1/\beta^m\) (block determinant formula). So \(\prod_i\lambda_i(\tilde C)=1/\beta^m\). The pairing: each eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(\tilde C\) is paired with \(1/(\beta\lambda)\); they come in pairs with product \(1/\beta\).

Stability check: for a saddle, need exactly \(m\) eigenvalues inside the unit circle. Under the pairing, this holds generically (unless some \(\lambda=1\) or \(1/\sqrt\beta\)).

Linearizing the DPP and the optimal decision rule L35 · Stab p.35

DPP linearization. The DPP is \(V(x)=\max_{y\in\Gamma(x)}[F(x,y)+\beta V(y)]\). Guess \(V(x)\approx V(\bar x)+V'(\bar x)\hat x+\frac12 V''(\bar x)\hat x^2\). The first-order condition in \(y\): \(F_y(x,g(x))+\beta V'(g(x))=0\). Linearizing in \(\hat x\):

$$[F_{yy}+\beta V''(\bar y)]\cdot g'(\bar x)\hat x=-F_{yx}\hat x.$$ $$g'(\bar x)=-\frac{F_{yx}}{F_{yy}+\beta V''(\bar y)}.$$

To find \(V''\): differentiate the DPP twice and use the EE. The result is a fixed-point (Riccati-type) equation in \(V''(\bar x)\).

Eigenvector formula for \(g'(\bar x)\). Partition the stable eigenvectors of the companion matrix \(\tilde C\) as \(\binom{P_{11}}{P_{12}}\in\mathbb R^{2m\times m}\), where \(P_{11}\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\) corresponds to the \(\hat x\) block and \(P_{12}\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\) to the \(\hat y\) block. Then:

$$g'(\bar x)=-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}.$$

Intuition: on the stable manifold, \(\hat y_t=g'(\bar x)\hat x_t\). The stable eigenvectors say \(\hat y=-(P_{11}^{-1}P_{12})\hat x\) on the saddle path. This is the discrete-time analogue of the continuous-time rule \(\hat\lambda=S\hat x\) with \(S=P_{12}P_{11}^{-1}\).

Stability check: eigenvalues of the closed-loop matrix \(\hat x_{t+1}=(P_{12}P_{11}^{-1})^{-1}\hat x_t\) should be inside the unit circle — they are the \(m\) stable eigenvalues of \(\tilde C\) by construction.

Common errors to avoid

  • Confusing "almost-reciprocal" (product \(=1/\beta\)) with "reciprocal" (product \(=1\)). The \(\beta\) factor matters: in the undiscounted case \(\beta=1\), you get true reciprocal pairs.
  • Applying the 1D formula \(\lambda_1\lambda_2=1/\beta\) to the \(n\)-D case. In \(n\) dimensions, \(\prod_i\lambda_i=1/\beta^m\); each pair has product \(1/\beta\), not the full product.
  • Forgetting to check \(P_{11}\) is invertible. The decision rule \(-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\) requires the state block of the stable eigenvectors to be square and invertible. Check this in numerical examples.
  • Mixing discrete and continuous companion matrices. The discrete \(\tilde C\) is \(2m\times2m\) with \(\det=1/\beta^m\). The continuous \(\mathcal H\) has eigenvalues summing to \(\rho\) in pairs; it has nothing like \(\det=1/\beta^m\).
  • Writing the wrong sign for \(g'(\bar x)\): it is \(-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\) (with a minus sign from the structure of the companion matrix), not \(+P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\).
