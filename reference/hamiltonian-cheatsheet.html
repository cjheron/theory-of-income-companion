Continuous-Time Optimal Control: Cheatsheet

Maximum principle and Hamiltonian; steady state and phase diagrams; Hamiltonian matrix and eigenvalue pairing; saddle path and Riccati equation; the Linear Quadratic Regulator. Lessons 36–39 · Slides · Glossary · Index
Exam must-know (final). Not on Midterm 1 or Midterm 2.

Be able to write from a blank page

  1. Set up the Hamiltonian: \(H(x,u,\lambda)=h(x,u)+\lambda'g(x,u)\). Three FOCs: \(H_u=0\), \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\), \(\dot x=H_\lambda\). TC: \(\lim e^{-\rho t}\lambda'x=0\).
  2. Neoclassical growth: \(H=U(c)+\lambda(f(k)-\delta k-c)\); FOC \(\lambda=U'(c)\); costate \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-\lambda(f'(k)-\delta)\); Keynes-Ramsey: \(\dot c/c=\sigma(f'(k)-\rho-\delta)\).
  3. Steady state: \(\dot x=\dot\lambda=0\). Modified golden rule: \(f'(\bar k)=\rho+\delta\). Costate: \(\bar\lambda=H_x/\rho\).
  4. Hamiltonian matrix \(\mathcal H=\begin{pmatrix}\bar H^*_{\lambda x}&\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}\\\rho I-\bar H^*_{xx}&-\bar H^*_{x\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\). Eigenvalue pairing: \(\mu\) is an eigenvalue \(\iff\) \(\rho-\mu\) is. For a \(2m\times2m\) system, \(m\) stable and \(m\) unstable eigenvalues (generic).
  5. Decision rule \(\hat\lambda=S\hat x\); \(S\) solves the ARE: \(S\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}S+S\bar H^*_{\lambda x}+\bar H^*_{x\lambda}S+\bar H^*_{xx}-\rho S=0\).
  6. LQR: \(h=\frac12(x'Qx+u'Ru+2x'Nu)\), \(\dot x=Ax+Bu\). FOC: \(u^*=-R^{-1}(N'x+B'\lambda)\). Constant \(\mathcal H\). Policy: \(u^*(x)=Gx=-R^{-1}(N'+B'S)x\). Value function: \(V^*(x)=x'Sx/2\).

Key facts and past-exam traps

  • Costate sign: \(\lambda(t)\ge0\) is NOT guaranteed in general — only when the state is a good (like capital). For pollutant stocks, \(\lambda\lt0\).
  • Modified vs. golden rule: \(f'(\bar k)=\rho+\delta\) (\(\bar k\) less than golden-rule \(k_G\)). Higher \(\rho\) → lower \(\bar k\). "Golden rule" sets \(\rho=0\) and maximizes long-run consumption.
  • Phase diagram arrows: \(\dot c=0\) locus is vertical at \(\bar k\). \(\dot k=0\) locus is \(c=f(k)-\delta k\), an inverted-U peaking at the golden rule \(k_G\). Above the \(\dot k=0\) locus: \(\dot k\lt0\). Right of \(\bar k\): \(\dot c>0\). Arrow directions determine saddle-path slope.
  • Pairing theorem (continuous time): eigenvalues of \(\mathcal H\) sum in pairs to \(\rho\). In discrete time, they multiply in pairs to \(1/\beta\). Different but analogous structures.
  • Riccati equation is quadratic in \(S\): the \(S\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}S\) term is quadratic. Two solutions; the stabilizing one makes closed-loop eigenvalues stable.
  • LQR value function: \(V^*(x)=x'Sx/2\) is exact (not just a local approximation) because the dynamics are globally linear. For nonlinear models, this is only a local approximation after log-linearization.
  • TC in continuous time: \(\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\rho T}\lambda'x=0\), not \(\lim\lambda'x=0\). The discount factor matters.

Exam format: continuous-time problems usually appear as one or two parts of a final exam question, often following a discrete-time problem on the same model. Expect to (1) write the Hamiltonian and FOCs, (2) derive the Keynes-Ramsey rule, (3) draw the phase diagram, (4) characterize the steady state, and sometimes (5) set up and interpret the Hamiltonian matrix. The LQR may appear as a "solve the following LQR problem" with an explicit ARE to solve.

The optimal control problem and the Hamiltonian L36 · Ham p.3–20

Problem. State \(x\in\mathbb R^m\), control \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), flow return \(h\), law of motion \(\dot x=g\):

$$V^*(x_0)=\max_{\{u(t)\}}\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}h(x,u)\,dt\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\dot x=g(x,u),\ x(0)=x_0.$$

Hamiltonian. Costate \(\lambda(t)\in\mathbb R^m\):

$$H(x,u,\lambda)=h(x,u)+\lambda'g(x,u).$$

Three first-order conditions:

  1. \(H_u=0\) — static optimality (control)
  2. \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\) — costate / EE
  3. \(\dot x=H_\lambda=g(x,u)\) — state equation

Transversality: \(\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\rho T}\lambda(T)'x(T)=0\).

Costate interpretation. \(\lambda(t)\) is the current-value shadow price of \(x\) at time \(t\). \(e^{-\rho t}\lambda(t)\) is the present-value shadow price at time 0. No-arbitrage: \(\rho\lambda=H_x+\dot\lambda\) (required return = dividend + capital gain).

Maximized Hamiltonian. \(H^*(x,\lambda)=\max_u H(x,u,\lambda)\), achieved at \(u^*(x,\lambda)\). Envelope derivatives:

$$H^*_x=H_x(x,u^*,\lambda),\qquad H^*_\lambda=g(x,u^*)=\dot x.$$

Properties: \(H^*\) is convex in \(\lambda\) (sup of linear functions), concave in \(x\) (under concavity of \(H\)).

Lagrangian proof sketch. Attach \(e^{-\rho t}\lambda\) to the constraint \(g-\dot x=0\); integrate by parts to move \(\dot x\); differentiate in \(u\) and \(x\). Stationarity in \(u\) gives \(H_u=0\); stationarity in \(x\) gives \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\); boundary term gives TC.

Neoclassical growth: steady state and phase diagram L37 · Ham p.21–24

Model: \(h=U(c)\), \(g=f(k)-\delta k-c\), \(\dot k=f(k)-\delta k-c\), \(\dot c/c=\sigma(f'(k)-\rho-\delta)\), \(\sigma=-U'/(cU'')\).

ConditionEquation
Modified golden rule\(f'(\bar k)=\rho+\delta\)
Resource constraint\(\bar c=f(\bar k)-\delta\bar k\)
Costate\(\bar\lambda=U'(\bar c)\)
Golden rule\(f'(k_G)=\delta\), \(\bar k\lt k_G\)

Log utility / Cobb-Douglas: \(U=\ln c\), \(f=Ak^\alpha\). \(\bar k=(A\alpha/(\rho+\delta))^{1/(1-\alpha)}\), \(\bar c=\bar k(\rho+(1-\alpha)\delta)/\alpha\), \(\bar\lambda=1/\bar c\). EIS \(\sigma=1\). KR rule: \(\dot c/c=A\alpha k^{\alpha-1}-\rho-\delta\).

Phase diagram in \((c,k)\) space.

  • \(\dot k=0\): \(c=f(k)-\delta k\). Inverted-U, peak at \(k_G\).
  • \(\dot c=0\): vertical line at \(k=\bar k\).
  • Above \(\dot k=0\) locus: \(\dot k\lt0\).
  • Right of \(\bar k\): \(\dot c>0\) (since \(f'\lt\rho+\delta\)).

Saddle paths: the two arms of the saddle — one approaching \((\bar k,\bar c)\) from the lower-left (sub-steady-state capital) and one from the upper-right (over-accumulated capital). Paths above the saddle: feasibility violated. Paths below: TC violated (over-accumulation).

Comparative statics: higher \(\rho\) → vertical \(\dot c=0\) locus shifts left → lower \(\bar k\), lower \(\bar c\). On impact, consumption jumps up and capital falls to new lower steady state.

Hamiltonian matrix, eigenvalue pairing, and the saddle path L38 · Ham p.25–52

The Hamiltonian matrix. Linearize \((\dot x,\dot\lambda)=(H^*_\lambda,\rho\lambda-H^*_x)\) at \((\bar x,\bar\lambda)\):

$$\mathcal H=\begin{pmatrix}\bar H^*_{\lambda x}&\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}\\\rho I-\bar H^*_{xx}&-\bar H^*_{x\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{2m\times2m}.$$

Eigenvalue pairing theorem. If \(\mu\) is an eigenvalue of \(\mathcal H\), so is \(\rho-\mu\) (same multiplicity). Proof: symplectic structure of \(\mathcal H\) implies characteristic polynomials at \(\mu\) and \(\rho-\mu\) are equal.

Saddle-point property. If no eigenvalue at \(\rho/2\): exactly \(m\) eigenvalues have \(\text{Re}(\mu)\lt0\), exactly \(m\) have \(\text{Re}(\mu)>0\). The optimal path lies on the \(m\)-dimensional stable manifold — uniquely determined by initial \(x_0\).

BlockExpressionSign (neoclassical)
\(A_{11}=\bar H^*_{\lambda x}\)\(f'(\bar k)-\delta=\rho\)\(\rho>0\)

\(A_{21}=\rho I-\bar H^*_{xx}\)\(\rho-\bar\lambda f''(\bar k)\)\(>0\)

Decision rule. On the saddle path: \(\hat\lambda(t)=S\hat x(t)\) where \(S\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\). Built from stable eigenvectors \(\binom{P_{11}}{P_{12}}\): \(S=P_{12}P_{11}^{-1}\). State dynamics: \(\dot{\hat x}=(A_{11}+A_{12}S)\hat x\), eigenvalues = stable ones of \(\mathcal H\).

Algebraic Riccati equation (ARE). Setting \(\dot\lambda=S\dot x\) along the optimal path:

$$SA_{12}S+SA_{11}-A_{22}S-A_{21}=0,$$

or in terms of \(H^*\) partials:

$$S\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}S+S\bar H^*_{\lambda x}+\bar H^*_{x\lambda}S+\bar H^*_{xx}-\rho S=0.$$

Scalar ARE (\(m=1\)): \(aS^2+(2b-\rho)S+c=0\) where \(a=\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}>0\), \(b=\bar H^*_{\lambda x}\), \(c=\bar H^*_{xx}\lt0\). Two roots; stabilizing root \(S_-\) makes closed-loop eigenvalue \(A_{11}+A_{12}S_-\lt0\).

The Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) L39 · Ham p.53–63

Problem.

$$\max\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}\frac{1}{2}(x'Qx+u'Ru+2x'Nu)\,dt\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\dot x=Ax+Bu,$$

with \(R\prec0\) (strict concavity in \(u\)), \(Q\preceq0\).

FOC: \(u^*=-R^{-1}(N'x+B'\lambda)\). Let \(\tilde A=A-BR^{-1}N'\), \(\tilde Q=Q-NR^{-1}N'\).

LQR Hamiltonian matrix (constant):

$$\mathcal H=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde A&-BR^{-1}B'\\-\tilde Q&\rho I-\tilde A'\end{pmatrix}.$$

LQR ARE:

$$-S(BR^{-1}B')S+S\tilde A+\tilde A'S-\rho S+\tilde Q=0.$$

Policy and value function:

$$u^*(x)=Gx,\quad G=-R^{-1}(N'+B'S),\quad V^*(x)=\tfrac12 x'Sx.$$

Scalar LQR (\(m=n=1\), \(N=0\)): \(\max\int e^{-\rho t}\frac12(qx^2+ru^2)\,dt\) s.t. \(\dot x=ax+bu\).

QuantityFormula
FOC\(u^*=-b\lambda/r\)
\(\mathcal H\)\(\begin{pmatrix}a&-b^2/r\\-q&\rho-a\end{pmatrix}\)

Key insight: for the LQR the Hamiltonian matrix is constant, so the saddle-path analysis is exact and global. The ARE has a unique stabilizing solution \(S\) (negative semidefinite for the maximization). Log-linearize a nonlinear model → LQR → extract \(S\) and linear policy functions (Blanchard-Kahn method).
