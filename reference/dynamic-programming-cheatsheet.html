Dynamic Programming: Deterministic and Stochastic

Exam must-know (final). Not on Midterm 1 or Midterm 2.

Deterministic DP: be able to write from a blank page

  1. Sequence problem: \(V^*(x_0)=\max_{\{x_{t+1}\}}\sum_{t=0}^\infty\beta^t F(x_t,x_{t+1})\) s.t. \(x_{t+1}\in\Gamma(x_t)\).
  2. Bellman equation: \(V(x)=\max_{y\in\Gamma(x)}\{F(x,y)+\beta V(y)\}\). The Principle of Optimality: \(V=V^*\).
  3. Benveniste–Scheinkman: \(V'(x)=F_x(x,g(x))\). Does NOT require \(g\) to be differentiable.
  4. Euler equation (from Bellman): \(F_y(x_t,x_{t+1})+\beta F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})=0\). For neoclassical growth: \(U'(c_t)=\beta U'(c_{t+1})f'(k_{t+1})\).
  5. Continuous-time Bellman (HJB): \(\rho V(x)=\max_u\{h(x,u)+V'(x)g(x,u)\}\). Costate identification: \(\lambda(t)=V'(x(t))\).
  6. Guess-and-verify: guess \(V(x)=a+b\log x\) or \(V(x)=-c/2\, x^2\); substitute into Bellman; match coefficients.

Stochastic DP: be able to write from a blank page

  1. Stochastic Bellman: \(V(x,z)=\max_{x'\in\Gamma(x,z)}\{F(x,x',z)+\beta E[V(x',z')\mid z]\}\). \(z\) is Markov.
  2. Stochastic Euler: \(0=F_y(x_t,x_{t+1},z_t)+\beta E_t[F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2},z_{t+1})]\).
  3. Certainty equivalence (LQ): policy \(y=G_x x+G_z z\); \(G_x\) is INDEPENDENT of \(\Sigma\) and \(A\). Same as the deterministic case with \(\Sigma=0\).
  4. Riccati equation: \(V_{xx}=F_{xx}-F_{xy}(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}F_{yx}\). Fixed point of \(T(P)=F_{xx}-F_{xy}(F_{yy}+\beta P)^{-1}F_{yx}\).
  5. Shock response: \(G_z=-(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}(F_{yz}+\beta V_{xz}A)\), where \(V_{xz}=F_{xz}+F_{xy}G_z\) simultaneously.

Key traps

  • Principle of Optimality ≠ global optimality: V* satisfies the Bellman, but other functions may too. Uniqueness requires CMT conditions.
  • Benveniste–Scheinkman: V' = F_x(x,g(x)), NOT F_x + F_y g'(x). The g'(x) terms cancel via the FOC.
  • CE in stochastic LQ: G_x is independent of Σ AND of A. Only G_z depends on A.

Sequence problem and the Bellman equation L40 · Det DP p.2–10

Sequence problem. State \(x_t\in X\), feasibility \(\Gamma:X\rightrightarrows X\), discount \(\beta\in(0,1)\):

$$V^*(x_0)=\max_{\{x_{t+1}\}_{t=0}^\infty}\sum_{t=0}^\infty\beta^t F(x_t,x_{t+1})\quad\text{s.t.}\ x_{t+1}\in\Gamma(x_t),\ x_0\ \text{given.}$$

Equivalent: \(V^*(x_0)=\max_{x^\infty\in\Pi^\infty(x_0)}u(\{x_{t+1}\})\) where \(u=\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^T\beta^t F\).

Bellman (recursive) problem. Find \(V:X\to\mathbb R\) s.t.:

$$V(x)=\max_{y\in\Gamma(x)}\{F(x,y)+\beta V(y)\}.$$

The maximizer is \(g(x)\): \(V(x)=F(x,g(x))+\beta V(g(x))\).

Principle of Optimality. Under the maintained existence conditions:

$$V^*(x)=V(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in X.$$

Once we know \(V\) and \(g\), we solve the problem for ANY \(x_0\in X\) in one step.

Sketch proof (bounded \(F\)). If \(|F|\leq B\): (1) \(|\beta^T V(x_T)|\leq\beta^T B/(1-\beta)\to0\); (2) substituting the Bellman recursion forward and taking \(T\to\infty\) gives \(V(x_0)\geq u(\cdot)\) for all plans; (3) equality is achieved by the plan \(x_{t+1}^*=g(x_t^*)\), giving \(V(x_0)\leq V^*(x_0)\). Conclusion: \(V=V^*\).

Advantages of DP. (1) Shocks: easy to handle via the state space. (2) Guess-and-verify: a corollary of CMT — any function in a T-invariant subspace is a candidate. (3) Computational: iterate \(T^n V_0\to V^*\) at geometric rate \(\beta\). (4) Discrete choice and non-convex problems. (5) Easier interpretation of shadow prices via the value function.

Contraction Mapping Theorem and Blackwell's conditions L41 · Det DP p.11–20

Setting. \(S=\{f:X\to\mathbb R\text{ continuous and bounded}\}\), \(\rho(f,g)=\|f-g\|=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)-g(x)|\). \(S\) is complete.

Contraction. \(T:S\to S\) is a contraction with modulus \(\beta\in(0,1)\) if \(\rho(Tf,Tg)\leq\beta\rho(f,g)\) for all \(f,g\in S\).

CMT. Unique fixed point \(s^*=Ts^*\); \(\rho(T^n s_0,s^*)\leq\beta^n\rho(s_0,s^*)\) for any \(s_0\in S\).

Corollary. If \(S'\subset S\) closed and \(TS'\subset S'\), then \(s^*\in S'\). Useful for showing V* inherits properties (monotone, concave, …).

Blackwell's sufficient conditions. Let \(T:S\to S\). If:

  1. Monotonicity: \(f\leq g\Rightarrow Tf\leq Tg\), and
  2. Discounting: \(T(f+a)\leq Tf+\beta a\) for all \(a\in\mathbb R_+\),

then T is a contraction with modulus \(\beta\).

Proof. Write \(f=g+(f-g)\leq g+\|f-g\|\). By monotonicity: \(Tf\leq T(g+\|f-g\|)\leq Tg+\beta\|f-g\|\). Reversing roles: \(Tg\leq Tf+\beta\|f-g\|\). Thus \(\|Tf-Tg\|\leq\beta\|f-g\|\). \(\square\)

Bellman operator: \((Tv)(x)=\max_{y\in\Gamma(x)}\{F(x,y)+\beta v(y)\}\). T maps S into S (Theorem of Max + continuity of \(\Gamma\)). Blackwell: (1) T monotone — if \(v\leq f\), old maximizer \(g\) gives \(Tv\leq Tf\); (2) T discounts: \(T(v+a)(x)=Tv(x)+\beta a\). Both satisfied \(\Rightarrow\) T is a contraction.

Properties of V*: monotonicity and concavity L41 · Det DP p.19–20

Monotonicity of V*. Assume \(F(\cdot,y)\) is increasing and \(\Gamma\) is increasing (\(x\leq x'\Rightarrow\Gamma(x)\subset\Gamma(x')\)). Then \(V^*\) is increasing.

Proof sketch. Let \(S'=\{\)increasing functions in \(S\}\). Show \(TS'\subset S'\): if \(f\) is increasing and \(x\leq x'\), then at the maximizer \(g(x)\in\Gamma(x)\subset\Gamma(x')\), \(Tf(x')=\max_{y\in\Gamma(x')}\{F(x',y)+\beta f(y)\}\geq F(x',g(x))+\beta f(g(x))\geq F(x,g(x))+\beta f(g(x))=Tf(x)\). By the Corollary, \(V^*\in S'\).

Concavity of V*. Assume X convex, \(\Gamma\) convex-valued (\(y\in\Gamma(x)\), \(y'\in\Gamma(x')\Rightarrow y^\theta\in\Gamma(x^\theta)\) for \(x^\theta=\theta x+(1-\theta)x'\)), and F concave in (x,y). Then \(V^*\) is concave.

Proof sketch. Let \(S''=\{\)concave functions in \(S\}\). Show \(TS''\subset S''\): for concave \(f\) and \(x^\theta=\theta x+(1-\theta)x'\), use the \(\theta\)-mixture plan \(y^\theta=\theta g(x)+(1-\theta)g(x')\in\Gamma(x^\theta)\). Then \(Tf(x^\theta)\geq F(x^\theta,y^\theta)+\beta f(y^\theta)\geq\theta[F(x,g(x))+\beta f(g(x))]+(1-\theta)[F(x',g(x'))+\beta f(g(x'))]=\theta Tf(x)+(1-\theta)Tf(x')\). By the Corollary, \(V^*\in S''\).

Strictly concave V*. If F strictly concave jointly in (x,y), then V* is strictly concave and g is single-valued (the maximizer is unique).

Benveniste–Scheinkman and Euler equations L42 · Det DP p.21–28

Benveniste–Scheinkman (B-S) theorem. Assume F is C¹ and the optimal policy satisfies \((x,g(x))\in\text{Int}(\text{Gr}(\Gamma))\). Then V* is differentiable and:

$$V'(x)=F_x(x,g(x)).$$

Key: does NOT require g to be differentiable. The \(F_y g'(x)\) terms cancel by the FOC.

Derivation (heuristic). Differentiate \(V(x)=F(x,g(x))+\beta V(g(x))\): \(V'=F_x+F_y g'+\beta V'(g)g'=F_x+[F_y+\beta V'(g)]g'=F_x\) (since FOC \(F_y+\beta V'(g)=0\)).

Euler equations from Bellman. FOC: \(F_y(x,g(x))+\beta V'(g(x))=0\). B-S: \(V'(x)=F_x(x,g(x))\). Combine: $$F_y(x_t,x_{t+1})+\beta F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})=0.$$ This is the same Euler equation derived from the sequence problem — the DP approach recovers it.

Neoclassical growth. \(F(k,k')=U(f(k)-k')\): \(F_y=-U'(c)\), \(F_x=U'(c)f'(k)\). Euler: \(U'(c_t)=\beta U'(c_{t+1})f'(k_{t+1})\) ✓

Guess-and-verify. A powerful method enabled by the Corollary of CMT: if V is in a T-invariant subspace, it must be V*.

Continuous-time Bellman (HJB) and the Maximum Principle L42 · Det DP p.32–39

HJB equation. State \(x\in X\), control \(u\in U\), flow return h, law of motion \(\dot x=g(x,u)\), discount \(\rho\):

$$\rho V(x)=\max_{u\in U}\{h(x,u)+V'(x)g(x,u)\}.$$

The optimal control \(u^*(x)\) satisfies the FOC: \(0=h_u(x,u^*(x))+V'(x)g_u(x,u^*(x))\).

Derivation. Write the Bellman equation with period length \(\Delta\). Taylor-expand \(V(x_{t+\Delta})\approx V(x_t)+V'(x_t)\Delta g(x,u)+o(\Delta)\). Divide by \(\Delta\) and let \(\Delta\to0\).

Connection to the Maximum Principle. Along the optimal path: \(\lambda(t)=V'(x(t))\). Differentiate \(\lambda=V'(x)\): \(\dot\lambda=V''(x)\dot x=V''g(x,u^*)\). Differentiating the HJB w.r.t. x and simplifying (using FOC to cancel \(u^*\) derivative terms):

$$\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-h_x(x,u^*)-\lambda g_x(x,u^*)=\rho\lambda-H_x.$$

This is exactly the costate equation of the Maximum Principle. So the Hamiltonian and HJB are equivalent formulations: \(\lambda(t)=V'(x(t))\) identifies the costate with the marginal value of the state.

ConceptHamiltonian (MP)HJB (DP)
Optimality\(H_u=0\)FOC in HJB
Costate\(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\)\(\lambda=V'(x)\) implicit

Stochastic Bellman equation and Markov processes L43 · Stoch DP p.2–14

Markov process \(z_t\in Z\).

  • Finite state: \(Q(z'|z)=\Pr\{z_{t+1}=z'\mid z_t=z\}\); matrix \(P\), \(P_{ij}=Q(\zeta_j|\zeta_i)\), rows sum to 1; \(t\)-step: \(Q_t=P^t\).
  • Continuous state: \(z_{t+1}=\mu(z_t)+\sigma(z_t)e_{t+1}\), \(e_t\) i.i.d.; \(F(a|z)=H((a-\mu(z))/\sigma(z))\).

Stochastic Bellman operator. Value function \(V:X\times Z\to\mathbb R\):

$$(TV)(x,z)=\max_{x'\in\Gamma(x,z)}\left\{F(x,x',z)+\beta\int_Z V(x',z')Q(dz'|z)\right\}.$$

Principle of Optimality: \(V=TV\iff V=V^*\). Proof similar to deterministic case.

Feller property. If \(f:Z\to\mathbb R\) is continuous, then \(Ef(\cdot)\equiv\int f(z')Q(dz'|\cdot)\) is continuous. Preserves continuity and monotonicity; used to show V*(x,z) is continuous and monotone in z.

Envelope and FOC. With \(g(x,z)\in\text{Int}\,\Gamma(x,z)\):

$$0=F_y(x,g(x,z),z)+\beta E[V_x(g(x,z),z')\mid z],\qquad V_x(x,z)=F_x(x,g(x,z),z).$$

Stochastic Euler equation. Combine FOC and envelope:

$$0=F_y(x_t,x_{t+1},z_t)+\beta E_t[F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2},z_{t+1})].$$

Transversality condition. \(\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\beta^T\int F_x(x_T,x_{T+1},z_T)\cdot x_T\cdot Q^{(T)}(dz^T|z_0)=0.\) Economic meaning: the present-value marginal value of the state times the state → 0 at infinity.

LQ stochastic DP and certainty equivalence L44 · Stoch DP p.15–26

LQ problem. Quadratic return in \((x,y,z)\), normalized around steady state:

$$F(x,y,z)=\tfrac{1}{2}[x'F_{xx}x+y'F_{yy}y+2x'F_{xy}y+2x'F_{xz}z+2y'F_{yz}z].$$

Exogenous shock: \(z_{t+1}=Az_t+\Sigma^{1/2}e_{t+1}\), \(e_t\) i.i.d., \(E[e_t]=0\), \(E[e_te_t']=I_k\).

Value function guess. \(V(x,z)=\frac12 x'V_{xx}x+x'V_{xz}z+\frac12 z'V_{zz}z+v_0\). The \(V_{zz}\) and \(v_0\) terms don't affect the choice of \(y\).

Optimal policy (linear decision rule). $$y=G_x x+G_z z,\quad G_x=-(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}F_{yx},\quad G_z=-(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}(F_{yz}+\beta V_{xz}A).$$

Certainty equivalence. \(G_x\) is independent of \(\Sigma\) (the variance of shocks) AND of \(A\) (the persistence of shocks). Proof: since \(E[e]=0\), the \(\Sigma\) terms vanish from the FOC and the x-coefficient equations are identical to the deterministic case.

Discrete-time Riccati equation. $$V_{xx}=F_{xx}-F_{xy}(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}F_{yx}.$$ Operator \(T(P)=F_{xx}-F_{xy}(F_{yy}+\beta P)^{-1}F_{yx}\). Fixed point: \(V_{xx}=P^*=T(P^*)\). \(T^m(P)\) = value function of \(m\)-horizon problem with terminal cost \(\frac12 x'Px\).

Solving for V_xz and G_z. Two linear equations: \(G_z=-(F_{yy}+\beta V_{xx})^{-1}(F_{yz}+\beta V_{xz}A)\) and \(V_{xz}=F_{xz}+F_{xy}G_z\). Given \(V_{xx}\), solve jointly for \((G_z,V_{xz})\).

LQ as approximation. As \(\Sigma\to0\): the LQ problem approximates a nonlinear model to first order around the steady state. The linear policy functions \((G_x,G_z)\) describe local first-order dynamics even when the true F is not quadratic.
