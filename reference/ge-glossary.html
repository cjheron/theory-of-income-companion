Glossary & Notation

The terms every lesson uses, defined once. Notation follows Alvarez's slides; where the decks clash, see notation traps.

Part I: The economy and equilibrium

\(L\), commodity space
Vector space of bundles; usually \(\mathbb{R}^m\), with \(m=\infty\) in OLG. Commodities are distinguished by physical characteristics, location, date and state of nature. L1
\(X^i\), \(u^i\)
Consumption possibility set and utility function of household \(i\in I\). In \(x\in X^i\), positive entries are purchases and negative entries are sales (e.g. labour). L1
\(Y^j\)
Technology (production possibility set) of firm \(j\in J\). In \(y\in Y^j\), positive entries are outputs and negative entries are inputs.
\(e^i\), \(\theta^i_j\)
Endowment of \(i\); share of firm \(j\) owned by \(i\), with \(\theta^i_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\theta^i_j=1\).
Feasible allocation
\(\{x^i,y^j\}\) with \(x^i\in X^i\), \(y^j\in Y^j\), and material balance \(\sum_i x^i=\sum_j y^j+\sum_i e^i\) (every good, every date and state).
\(p\), \(p\cdot x\)
Price vector and the value of bundle \(x\) (inner product). Only relative prices matter, so we normalize one. L2
Competitive equilibrium (CE)
\((p,\{\bar x^i,\bar y^j\})\): each \(\bar y^j\) maximizes \(p\cdot y\) on \(Y^j\), each \(\bar x^i\) maximizes \(u^i\) subject to \(p\cdot x\le p\cdot e^i+\sum_j\theta^i_j\pi^j\), and the allocation is feasible. L2
Walras' law
With binding budgets, \(p\cdot z(p)=0\) at every \(p\), where \(z\) is aggregate excess demand. So \(m-1\) clearing conditions suffice.
Pareto optimal (PO)
A feasible allocation that no other feasible allocation Pareto-dominates, i.e. makes everyone weakly better off and someone strictly better off. L3
Local non-satiation (LNS)
For every \(x\in X^i\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(\hat x\in B_\varepsilon(x)\cap X^i\) with \(u^i(\hat x)>u^i(x)\). No bliss points and no thick indifference curves. L3
First Welfare Theorem (1WT)
Under LNS, every CE allocation is PO. It needs no convexity. In infinite economies it also needs \(p\cdot\sum_i\bar x^i<\infty\). L4, L12
Upper contour set
\(\{x\in X^i: u^i(x)\ge u^i(\bar x)\}\). It is strictly convex under strict quasi-concavity (Assumption HH).
Aggregate production set \(Y\)
\(Y=\sum_j Y^j\) (set sum). Assumption FF: \(Y\) is convex.
Second Welfare Theorem (2WT)
Under HH and FF, plus one non-satiated household, any PO allocation is supported by some \(p\ne0\): firms maximize profits and households minimize expenditure (a quasi-equilibrium). L6
Separating hyperplane theorem
Two disjoint convex sets (here \(A\) and \(\operatorname{Int}B\)) can be separated by a hyperplane \(\{x: p\cdot x = c\}\), \(p\ne0\).

Part II–III: Overlapping generations

Generation \(i\)
Agents born at date \(i\ge1\) live for dates \(i,i+1\) with utility \(v^i(x_i^i,x_{i+1}^i)\). The initial old (\(i=0\)) live only at \(t=1\), with \(u^0=x_1^0\). L8
\(\bar e_t\)
Aggregate endowment at \(t\): \(e_t^{t-1}+e_t^t\). Feasibility: \(x_t^{t-1}+x_t^t=\bar e_t\).
\(r_t\)
Net interest rate, \(1/(1+r_t)=p_{t+1}/p_t\), so \(p_t=1/\prod_{sL9
\(s(r;\alpha,\beta)\)
Savings of the young: \(\beta(1-\alpha)-(1-\beta)\alpha/(1+r)\) in the log example. Equilibrium sets \(s=0\), so \(\bar r=(\alpha-\beta)/(\beta(1-\alpha))\).
Symmetric allocation
\((c_y,c_o)\), the same for every generation; feasible iff \(c_y+c_o=1\) (no growth). The best one for the young is \((1-\beta,\beta)\). L10
Dynamic inefficiency
A CE that is Pareto-dominated, e.g. by intergenerational transfers. It arises when \(\bar r<0\), or with growth when \(1+\bar r<(1+g)(1+n)\). L13
Pay-as-you-go (PAYG) social security
Tax each young agent \(\tau\) and transfer the proceeds to the current old. It is balanced every period. With population growth each old agent receives \((1+n)\tau\). L11
Value of aggregate consumption
\(p\cdot\sum_i\bar x^i\). The 1WT proof needs it finite. In the OLG example it equals \(\sum_t p_t\), which diverges iff \(\bar r\le0\). L12
Ricardian equivalence
Changing the timing of taxes while preserving each generation's present value leaves consumption and prices unchanged. L14, L20
\(B_t\)
Government assets in Class Note III (\(B_t<0\) is debt). Asset market clearing: \(B_t+s_t=0\) (endowment) or \(B_t+s_t=K_{t+1}\) (with capital).
Golden rule
The steady-state capital stock that maximizes steady-state consumption: \(F'(K)=\delta\) gross, or \(r=n\) with growth (\(r=0\) without). L15

Part IV: Perpetual youth (Blanchard–Yaari)

\(p\)
Death rate (hazard), not a price; expected lifetime \(1/p\). Cohort born at \(s\) has size \(N(s,t)=pe^{-p(t-s)}\) at \(t\); total population is 1. L17
Actuarially fair annuity
Wealth held with an insurer earns \(r+p\) while alive and is forfeited at death. The insurer breaks even.
\(\theta\)
Pure rate of time preference. The effective discount rate is \(\theta+p\).
\(R(t,z)\)
Price at \(t\) of a good at \(z\) for a surviving agent: \(\exp(-\int_t^z[r(\mu)+p]d\mu)\).
\(h\), \(v\); \(H\), \(V\)
Individual human and non-human wealth; aggregates over cohorts. With log utility, \(c=(\theta+p)(h+v)\) and \(C=(\theta+p)(H+V)\).
\(\alpha\) (BY)
Rate at which labour income declines with age: \(y(s,t)=aY(t)e^{-\alpha(t-s)}\) with \(a=(p+\alpha)/p\). L18
Aggregate system
\(\dot C=(r+\alpha-\theta)C-(p+\alpha)(p+\theta)V\), \(\dot V=rV+Y-C\). Endowment economy: \(r=\theta-\alpha\). L19
\(F(K)\) (BY)
Net output \(F(K,1)-\delta K\), so \(r=F'(K)\) and \(Y=F(K)-F'(K)K\).
