Theory of Income · Reference

Glossary & Notation

The terms every lesson uses, defined once. Notation follows Alvarez's slides; where the decks clash, see notation traps.

Part I: The economy and equilibrium

\(L\), commodity space
Vector space of bundles; usually \(\mathbb{R}^m\), with \(m=\infty\) in OLG. Commodities are distinguished by physical characteristics, location, date and state of nature. L1
\(X^i\), \(u^i\)
Consumption possibility set and utility function of household \(i\in I\). In \(x\in X^i\), positive entries are purchases and negative entries are sales (e.g. labour). L1
\(Y^j\)
Technology (production possibility set) of firm \(j\in J\). In \(y\in Y^j\), positive entries are outputs and negative entries are inputs.
\(e^i\), \(\theta^i_j\)
Endowment of \(i\); share of firm \(j\) owned by \(i\), with \(\theta^i_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\theta^i_j=1\).
Feasible allocation
\(\{x^i,y^j\}\) with \(x^i\in X^i\), \(y^j\in Y^j\), and material balance \(\sum_i x^i=\sum_j y^j+\sum_i e^i\) (every good, every date and state).
\(p\), \(p\cdot x\)
Price vector and the value of bundle \(x\) (inner product). Only relative prices matter, so we normalize one. L2
Competitive equilibrium (CE)
\((p,\{\bar x^i,\bar y^j\})\): each \(\bar y^j\) maximizes \(p\cdot y\) on \(Y^j\), each \(\bar x^i\) maximizes \(u^i\) subject to \(p\cdot x\le p\cdot e^i+\sum_j\theta^i_j\pi^j\), and the allocation is feasible. L2
Walras' law
With binding budgets, \(p\cdot z(p)=0\) at every \(p\), where \(z\) is aggregate excess demand. So \(m-1\) clearing conditions suffice.
Pareto optimal (PO)
A feasible allocation that no other feasible allocation Pareto-dominates, i.e. makes everyone weakly better off and someone strictly better off. L3
Local non-satiation (LNS)
For every \(x\in X^i\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(\hat x\in B_\varepsilon(x)\cap X^i\) with \(u^i(\hat x)>u^i(x)\). No bliss points and no thick indifference curves. L3
First Welfare Theorem (1WT)
Under LNS, every CE allocation is PO. It needs no convexity. In infinite economies it also needs \(p\cdot\sum_i\bar x^i\lt \infty\). L4, L12
Upper contour set
\(\{x\in X^i: u^i(x)\ge u^i(\bar x)\}\). It is strictly convex under strict quasi-concavity (Assumption HH).
Aggregate production set \(Y\)
\(Y=\sum_j Y^j\) (set sum). Assumption FF: \(Y\) is convex.
Second Welfare Theorem (2WT)
Under HH and FF, plus one non-satiated household, any PO allocation is supported by some \(p\ne0\): firms maximize profits and households minimize expenditure (a quasi-equilibrium). L6
Separating hyperplane theorem
Two disjoint convex sets (here \(A\) and \(\operatorname{Int}B\)) can be separated by a hyperplane \(\{x: p\cdot x = c\}\), \(p\ne0\).
Arrow's remark / cheapest point
If \(\bar x^i\) is not the cheapest point in \(X^i\) (some \(\tilde x^i\) has \(p\cdot\tilde x^i\lt p\cdot\bar x^i\)), expenditure minimization implies utility maximization, so the quasi-equilibrium is a CE. L7

Part II–III: Overlapping generations

Generation \(i\)
Agents born at date \(i\ge1\) live for dates \(i,i+1\) with utility \(v^i(x_i^i,x_{i+1}^i)\). The initial old (\(i=0\)) live only at \(t=1\), with \(u^0=x_1^0\). L8
\(\bar e_t\)
Aggregate endowment at \(t\): \(e_t^{t-1}+e_t^t\). Feasibility: \(x_t^{t-1}+x_t^t=\bar e_t\).
\(r_t\)
Net interest rate, \(1/(1+r_t)=p_{t+1}/p_t\), so \(p_t=1/\prod_{s\lt t}(1+r_s)\) with \(p_1=1\). L9
\(s(r;\alpha,\beta)\)
Savings of the young: \(\beta(1-\alpha)-(1-\beta)\alpha/(1+r)\) in the log example. Equilibrium sets \(s=0\), so \(\bar r=(\alpha-\beta)/(\beta(1-\alpha))\).
Symmetric allocation
\((c_y,c_o)\), the same for every generation; feasible iff \(c_y+c_o=1\) (no growth). The best one for the young is \((1-\beta,\beta)\). L10
Dynamic inefficiency
A CE that is Pareto-dominated, e.g. by intergenerational transfers. It arises when \(\bar r<0\), or with growth when \(1+\bar r<(1+g)(1+n)\). L13
Pay-as-you-go (PAYG) social security
Tax each young agent \(\tau\) and transfer the proceeds to the current old. It is balanced every period. With population growth each old agent receives \((1+n)\tau\). L11
Value of aggregate consumption
\(p\cdot\sum_i\bar x^i\). The 1WT proof needs it finite. In the OLG example it equals \(\sum_t p_t\), which diverges iff \(\bar r\le0\). L12
Ricardian equivalence
Changing the timing of taxes while preserving each generation's present value leaves consumption and prices unchanged. L14, L20
\(B_t\)
Government assets in Class Note III (\(B_t<0\) is debt). Asset market clearing: \(B_t+s_t=0\) (endowment) or \(B_t+s_t=K_{t+1}\) (with capital).
Golden rule
The steady-state capital stock that maximizes steady-state consumption: \(F'(K)=\delta\) gross, or \(r=n\) with growth (\(r=0\) without). L15 Contrast the modified golden rule \(\beta f'(\bar k)=1\) of the planner's problem (Part VI). L27

Part IV: Perpetual youth (Blanchard–Yaari)

\(p\)
Death rate (hazard), not a price; expected lifetime \(1/p\). Cohort born at \(s\) has size \(N(s,t)=pe^{-p(t-s)}\) at \(t\); total population is 1. L17
Actuarially fair annuity
Wealth held with an insurer earns \(r+p\) while alive and is forfeited at death. The insurer breaks even.
\(\theta\)
Pure rate of time preference. The effective discount rate is \(\theta+p\).
\(R(t,z)\)
Price at \(t\) of a good at \(z\) for a surviving agent: \(\exp(-\int_t^z[r(\mu)+p]d\mu)\).
\(h\), \(v\); \(H\), \(V\)
Individual human and non-human wealth; aggregates over cohorts. With log utility, \(c=(\theta+p)(h+v)\) and \(C=(\theta+p)(H+V)\).
\(\alpha\) (BY)
Rate at which labour income declines with age: \(y(s,t)=aY(t)e^{-\alpha(t-s)}\) with \(a=(p+\alpha)/p\). L18
Aggregate system
\(\dot C=(r+\alpha-\theta)C-(p+\alpha)(p+\theta)V\), \(\dot V=rV+Y-C\). Endowment economy: \(r=\theta-\alpha\). L19
\(F(K)\) (BY)
Net output \(F(K,1)-\delta K\), so \(r=F'(K)\) and \(Y=F(K)-F'(K)K\).

Part V: Aggregation and the social planner (Class Note 3)

Cardinal vs ordinal property
An ordinal property survives every strictly increasing transformation of \(u^i\), so it depends only on preferences (and demand). Quasi-concavity is ordinal; concavity is cardinal: \(e^{u}\) of a concave \(u\) need not be concave. L21
Utility possibility set \(U\)
\(\{u\in\mathbb{R}^I: u_i\le u^i(x^i)\ \forall i\) for some feasible allocation\(\}\). It is convex under HH, FF and concave \(u^i\) (assumption CC). PO utility vectors lie on its upper boundary. L21
Downward comprehensive
\(u\in U\) and \(u'\le u\) (component by component) imply \(u'\in U\). This is what forces supporting weights to be non-negative.
Pareto (welfare) weights \(\lambda\)
Non-negative, nonzero \(\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^I_+\) in the planner objective \(\sum_i\lambda_iu^i\). Every PO allocation solves the planner problem for some \(\lambda\) (under HH, CC, FF). With \(\lambda\gg0\), every solution is PO; with zero weights, a solution need not be PO. L21
Planner problem \(W\)
\(\max_{\{x^i,y^j\}}\sum_i\lambda_iu^i(x^i)\) subject to feasibility.
Representative agent / representative utility \(u(x;\lambda)\)
\(\max_{\{x^i\in X^i\}}\sum_i\lambda_iu^i(x^i)\) s.t. \(\sum_ix^i=x\). It is concave in \(x\) when the \(X^i\) are convex and the \(u^i\) concave, and HD1 in \(\lambda\). The weights that reproduce a CE are the Negishi weights. L22
Pure exchange economy
No production: \(Y=-\mathbb{R}^m_+\) (free disposal only), so feasibility is \(\sum_ix^i\le\sum_ie^i\equiv\bar e\).
Shadow value \(\gamma_l\)
The multiplier on feasibility of good \(l\) in the planner problem. It equals the marginal value of one more unit of \(\bar e_l\) to the planner, \(\partial u(\bar e;\lambda)/\partial x_l\). Planner FOC: \(\lambda_iu^i_l=\gamma_l\). It depends on \(\lambda\) in general.
Marginal utility of income \(\mu_i\)
The multiplier on \(i\)'s budget constraint. CE FOC: \(u^i_l=\mu_ip_l\). By the envelope theorem it is the utility gain from one more unit of numeraire income. Richer agents have lower \(\mu_i\). L23
Negishi weights / Negishi method
The planner weights that reproduce a CE: \(\lambda_i=\kappa/\mu_i\), with \(\gamma=\kappa p\). Matching the planner and household FOCs this way proves both welfare theorems (interior, differentiable case). Not \(\lambda_i=\mu_i\). L23
Envy-free allocation
Feasible \(\{x^i\}\) with \(u^i(x^i)\ge u^i(x^j)\) for all \(i,j\). The CE from equal endowments \(e^i=\bar e/I\) is envy-free (identical budget sets); equal endowments are sufficient, not necessary.
Homogeneous of degree \(k\) (HD\(k\))
\(h(\eta x)=\eta^kh(x)\) for all \(\eta>0\). If \(h\) is HD1, its partial derivatives are HD0: \(h_l(\eta x)=h_l(x)\). More generally, the partials of an HD\(k\) function are HD\((k-1)\). L24, L31
Euler's theorem
If \(h\) is differentiable and HD1, then \(h(x)=\sum_lx_l\,h_l(x)=\nabla h(x)\cdot x\).
Homothetic
Preferences representable as \(g(h(x))\) with \(g\) strictly increasing and \(h\) HD1. Indifference curves are radial blow-ups of each other, MRS is constant along rays, and Engel curves are rays through the origin.
Gorman aggregation / Gorman form
Equilibrium relative prices (equivalently \(\gamma_l/\gamma_k\)) do not depend on the distribution of wealth or Pareto weights, so a representative agent evaluated at \(\bar e\) prices the economy. It holds for \(u^i=g^i(h(x))\) with common HD1 \(h\): \(p\propto\nabla h(\bar e)\). More generally (Gorman polar form) Engel curves are linear with a slope common to all agents. L24, L25
Subsistence / translation vector \(\theta^i\)
An agent-specific shift of the origin: \(u^i(x)=g(H(x-\theta^i))\). If \(\theta^i_l>0\), good \(l\) is a necessity for \(i\). Aggregation then works on net aggregate endowment \(\bar e-\bar\theta\), with \(\bar\theta=\sum_i\theta^i\). L25
Stone–Geary
\(u^i=\sum_l\beta_l\log(x_l-\theta^i_l)\). It is non-homothetic and non-identical when the \(\theta^i\) differ, yet it aggregates if \(\beta\) is common: \(\gamma_l/\gamma_k=\frac{\beta_l(\bar e_k-\bar\theta_k)}{\beta_k(\bar e_l-\bar\theta_l)}\). It is the \(\sigma=1\) case of translated CES.
Satiation (bliss) point
A bundle beyond which more is not better, e.g. \(\theta^i\) in \(-\sum_l\pi_l(\theta^i_l-x_l)^{1+\sigma}/(1+\sigma)\) on \(x\le\theta^i\). It violates LNS at \(\theta^i\). That family still aggregates (needs \(\bar e\lt\bar\theta\)).

Part VI: Euler equations and transversality (folder 4)

Sequence problem \([X,\Gamma,F,\beta]\)
\(V^*(x_0)=\max_{\{x_{t+1}\}}\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tF(x_t,x_{t+1})\) s.t. \(x_{t+1}\in\Gamma(x_t)\), with \(x_0\) given. The choice is the whole path of next-period states. L26
Feasibility correspondence \(\Gamma\); graph \(\mathrm{Gr}(\Gamma)\)
\(\Gamma(x)\subseteq X\) is the set of states reachable next period from \(x\) (NGM: \([0,f(k)]\), from \(c\ge0\)). \(\mathrm{Gr}(\Gamma)=\{(x,y):x\in X,\ y\in\Gamma(x)\}\) is the domain of \(F\). L26
Period return \(F(x,y)\)
The payoff in a period as a function of today's and tomorrow's state, after the controls have been substituted out (NGM: \(U(f(k)-k')\)). It is not a production function. L26
State vs control
A state is predetermined at \(t\) (\(k_t\), \(h_t\), assets). A control is chosen at \(t\) (\(c_t\), \(i_t\), hours). Control/state form \([X,U,h,g,\beta]\): \(x_{t+1}=g(x_t,u_t)\), \(u_t\in U\), with return \(h(x,u)\). It maps to \(F(x,y)=\max_u\{h(x,u):y=g(x,u)\}\). L26
Euler equation (EE)
\(F_y(x_t,x_{t+1})+\beta F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})=0\): the marginal cost of one more unit of \(x_{t+1}\) equals its discounted marginal return next period. It is a second-order difference equation \(x_{t+2}=\psi(x_{t+1},x_t)\), well defined when \(F_{xy}\ne0\). NGM: \(U'(c_t)=\beta U'(c_{t+1})f'(k_{t+1})\). L27, L28
Transversality condition (TC)
\(\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta^tF_x(x_t,x_{t+1})\cdot x_t=0\): the discounted shadow value of the stock left "at infinity" is zero. It is the terminal condition that, with \(x_0\), selects the optimal path among EE solutions, ruling out over-accumulation. L27, L29
Sufficiency theorem (EE + TC)
If \(F\) is concave, \(F_x\ge0\) along the path and \(X=\mathbb R^m_+\), a path satisfying the EE and TC is optimal (Stokey–Lucas Thm 4.15). The proof uses the concavity inequality, telescoping with the EE, and the TC to kill the tail. Necessity needs interiority instead of concavity, and for the TC it is heuristic in this course. L27, L28
Steady state (dynamic)
\(\bar x\) with \(F_y(\bar x,\bar x)+\beta F_x(\bar x,\bar x)=0\), so \(x_{t+1}=\bar x\) is optimal from \(x_0=\bar x\). It is unchanged by \(U\circ F\) or by adding \(-\frac b2(y-x)^2\). L27
Modified golden rule
The NGM steady state \(\beta f'(\bar k)=1\), i.e. \(G_k(\bar k)=\rho+\delta\). It lies below the golden rule \(G_k=\delta\) because of impatience, and it does not depend on \(U\). L27
Policy function \(g\)
\(x_{t+1}=g(x_t)\), the optimal choice of next period's state. In policy form the EE reads \(F_y(x,g(x))+\beta F_x(g(x),g(g(x)))=0\) for all \(x\). Examples: \(g(k)=\alpha\beta k^\alpha\) (log/CD, \(\delta=1\)); \(g(x)=\gamma_1x\) (quadratic cost). L28, L30
Shooting algorithm
Given \(x_0\), guess \(x_1\), generate the path from \(x_{t+2}=\psi(x_{t+1},x_t)\), and revise \(x_1\) (e.g. by bisection) until the path satisfies the TC, i.e. converges to the steady state. L28
Saddle path
The unique path through \(x_0\) that converges to the steady state. Nearby EE paths diverge (in the NGM, \(k\to0\) or \(c\to0\) with \(k\to k_{\max}\)), and the TC selects the saddle path. L28
No-Ponzi / over-accumulation
Two ways a path can fail at infinity. A Ponzi scheme borrows without limit, and is ruled out by the constraint set (here \(X=\mathbb R_+\)). Over-accumulation saves "too much" forever, and is ruled out by the TC. Example: \(\tilde c=c^*-\Delta\) with \(\beta(1+r)=1\) satisfies the EE, yet the TC term → \((1+r)U'(\tilde c)\Delta/r>0\). L29
Homogeneous dynamic problem
\(X\) is a cone, \(\Gamma\) is HD1, and \(F\) is HD1 (or HD\((1-\gamma)\)). The optimal policy is then HD1, \(y=\bar gx\), with EE \(F_y(1,\bar g)+\beta\bar g^{-\gamma}F_x(1,\bar g)=0\) and TC \(|\beta\bar g^{1-\gamma}|\lt1\) (\(\gamma=0\) for HD1). L31
Balanced growth
All stocks and flows grow at a common constant factor \(\bar g\). It is the solution form of homogeneous problems, which have no transition dynamics. L31, L32
AK model
Linear technology \(c_t+k_{t+1}=Ak_t\) with CRRA utility. The growth factor is \(\bar g=(\beta A)^{1/\gamma}\), and the TC \(\iff\bar g\lt A\iff\) consumption is positive. L31
Adjustment cost function \(a(g)\)
Total resources used per unit of capital to grow capital by the factor \(g=k'/k\): \(a(g)=g-(1-\delta)+\phi(g-(1-\delta))\), i.e. investment plus the installation cost \(\phi\). It is assumed positive and strictly convex, with \(a'(1)=1\). Quadratic version (slide 32): \(-\frac b2(y-x)^2\). L30, L32
Tobin's q
Market value of the firm per unit of installed capital. With \(F=Ax-a(y/x)x\): \(q=V(k)/k=\frac{A-a(\bar g)}{1-\beta\bar g}=\frac{a'(\bar g)}{\beta}\) (cum-dividend). Observable, it forecasts the growth of capital. L32
Marginal vs average q
Marginal q is \(V'(k)\), the value of one more installed unit. Average q is \(V(k)/k\). They coincide when \(V\) is HD1 (CRS technology and adjustment costs, price-taking firm), the Hayashi (1982) condition. L32
Local stability (discrete time)
A steady state \(\bar x\) is locally stable if deviations \(\hat x_t=x_t-\bar x\) governed by the linearized EE converge to 0. The linearized system has characteristic roots \(\lambda_i\); stable iff \(|\lambda_i|\lt1\). For the saddle path, exactly \(m\) roots must be inside the unit circle. L33
Almost-reciprocal pairs (discrete time)
Roots of the characteristic polynomial of the linearized EE come in pairs \((\lambda_i,\,1/(\beta\lambda_i))\) with product \(1/\beta\). In 1D: if \(|\lambda_1|\lt1\) then \(|\lambda_2|=1/(\beta|\lambda_1|)>1\). In \(n\)-D: \(\prod_i\lambda_i=1/\beta^m\), where \(m\) is the state dimension. L33
Optimal decision rule (discrete time, linearized)
\(g'(\bar x)=-P_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\), where \(\binom{P_{11}}{P_{12}}\) are the \(m\) stable eigenvectors of the companion matrix, partitioned into the \(x\) and \(y\) blocks. The continuous-time analogue is \(\hat\lambda=S\hat x\) with \(S=P_{12}P_{11}^{-1}\). L35
Current-value Hamiltonian \(H(x,u,\lambda)\)
\(H=h(x,u)+\lambda'g(x,u)\): the present-value-adjusted Lagrangian that converts a continuous-time control problem to a family of static problems. The costate \(\lambda(t)\) is the shadow price of the state at time \(t\). L36
Costate variable \(\lambda(t)\)
The continuous-time Lagrange multiplier on the law of motion \(\dot x=g\). \(e^{-\rho t}\lambda(t)\) is the present-value shadow price of the state at time \(t\). Satisfies the costate equation \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\). L36
Maximum principle (Pontryagin)
Necessary (and under concavity, sufficient) conditions for a continuous-time optimal control problem: (1) \(H_u=0\); (2) \(\dot\lambda=\rho\lambda-H_x\); (3) \(\dot x=H_\lambda=g\); (4) TC: \(\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\rho T}\lambda'x=0\). L36
Keynes-Ramsey rule
The continuous-time Euler equation for the neoclassical growth model: \(\dot c/c=\sigma(c)(f'(k)-\rho-\delta)\), where \(\sigma(c)=-U'/(cU'')\) is the EIS. Obtained by differentiating \(\lambda=U'(c)\) and substituting the costate equation. L36
Modified golden rule (continuous time)
The steady-state condition \(f'(\bar k)=\rho+\delta\): the net return on capital equals the discount rate. The golden rule (zero discounting) gives \(f'(k_G)=\delta\), producing higher capital. \(\bar k\lt k_G\) because of impatience (\(\rho>0\)). L37
Hamiltonian matrix \(\mathcal H\)
The \(2m\times2m\) matrix governing the linearized state-costate system \(\dot{\hat z}=\mathcal H\hat z\). Blocks: \(\bar H^*_{\lambda x}\), \(\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}\), \(\rho I-\bar H^*_{xx}\), \(-\bar H^*_{x\lambda}\). Its eigenvalues come in pairs \((\mu,\rho-\mu)\) summing to \(\rho\). L38
Eigenvalue pairing (continuous time)
If \(\mu\) is an eigenvalue of \(\mathcal H\) then \(\rho-\mu\) is also one. Analogous to the discrete-time almost-reciprocal pairs \((\lambda,1/(\beta\lambda))\). For the saddle path, exactly \(m\) eigenvalues have \(\text{Re}(\mu)\lt0\). L38
Algebraic Riccati equation (ARE)
The matrix equation \(S\bar H^*_{\lambda\lambda}S+S\bar H^*_{\lambda x}+\bar H^*_{x\lambda}S+\bar H^*_{xx}-\rho S=0\) satisfied by the saddle-path matrix \(S\) in the optimal rule \(\hat\lambda=S\hat x\). Quadratic in \(S\); two solutions, one stabilizing. For the LQR, it has a unique positive-semidefinite stabilizing solution. L38
Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR)
A continuous-time optimal control problem with quadratic return \(h=\frac12(x'Qx+u'Ru+2x'Nu)\) and linear law of motion \(\dot x=Ax+Bu\). The Hamiltonian matrix is constant. The optimal policy is globally linear \(u^*=Gx\), and the value function is exactly quadratic \(V^*(x)=x'Sx/2\). L39
