Euler Equations & Transversality: Cheatsheet

Discrete-time deterministic dynamic problems: the sequence problem, Euler equations and transversality (sufficiency and necessity), steady states, shooting, closed-form decision rules, homogeneous problems and AK growth, adjustment costs and Tobin's q. Lessons 26–32 · Slides · Glossary · Index
Exam must-know (MT2 / final). Not on Midterm 1.

Be able to write from a blank page

  1. Any model as \(F(x,y)\) and \(\Gamma(x)\): substitute \(c\) (and \(i\)) out with the law of motion, and get \(\Gamma\) from \(c\ge0\). Then list \(F_x>0\), \(F_y\lt 0\), \(F_{xx}\lt 0\), \(F_{yy}\lt 0\), \(F_{xx}F_{yy}-F_{xy}^2>0\) and the sign of \(F_{xy}\).
  2. EE: \(F_y(x_t,x_{t+1})+\beta F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})=0\). In policy form: \(F_y(x,g(x))+\beta F_x(g(x),g(g(x)))=0\) for all \(x\).
  3. TC: \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta^tF_x(x_t,x_{t+1})\cdot x_t=0\). Steady state: \(F_y(\bar x,\bar x)+\beta F_x(\bar x,\bar x)=0\).
  4. The sufficiency proof in five lines (below), with its three hypotheses.
  5. Linear-policy guess \(x_{t+1}=\gamma x_t\) → a quadratic \(Q(\gamma)\): \(Q(0)>0\), \(Q(1)\lt 0\), convex. Pick the root in \((0,1)\); the other violates the TC.
  6. \(s=\alpha\beta\) (log, Cobb–Douglas, \(\delta=1\)); constant saving rate with CES/CRRA iff \(\sigma=\rho\), with \(s=(\alpha\beta)^{\sigma}\).

Past-exam traps (Fall 2005, Fall 2016, 2017/18, Fall 2025)

  • Steady-state invariance is the favourite twist. At \((\bar x,\bar x)\), a common factor cancels (\(U\circ F\), 2025 MT1 Q6), an added term vanishes (\(-\frac b2(y-x)^2\), 2016 MT2 Q2), and \(u\) drops out of \(\beta f'(\bar k)=1\) (2005 T/F Q6: True). The dynamics do change.
  • "Concavity is needed for necessity of the TC" (2005 T/F Q9): False. Concavity is used for sufficiency (slide 14), and the necessity argument (slide 23) never uses it.
  • Concavity checks need \(F_{yy}\) and the determinant. Circulating answers to 2025 MT1 QII.10–11 check only \(F_x,F_y,F_{xx},F_{xy}\).
  • "The EE can't be derived without the envelope condition": False. A circulating T/F bank says True, but slides 19–22 perturb the sequence problem directly.
  • CES/CRRA: a circulating study summary says "only log utility, σ=0". Correct: \(\sigma=\rho\), and log is \(\sigma=1\).
  • \(c_t+k_{t+1}=Ak_t^\alpha\) is 100% depreciation, not "no depreciation", which is a common mislabel.
  • 2017/18 core Q3, \(c\lt 0\): the root closest to 0 is decreasing in \(\alpha=a/c\), moving toward −1 as \(\alpha\uparrow-2\). A circulating answer says increasing.
  • With the quadratic guess \(V=-\frac c2x^2\): \(\beta c^2+(a+(1-\beta)b)c-ab=0\). A circulating review drops the \(\beta bc\) term.

The problem \([X,\Gamma,F,\beta]\) L26 · slides 3–5

  • The state space is \(X\). Next period's state is \(y\in\Gamma(x)\) (the feasibility correspondence), and \(\mathrm{Gr}(\Gamma)=\{(x,y):x\in X,\ y\in\Gamma(x)\}\).
  • The period return is \(F:\mathrm{Gr}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb R\), and the discount factor is \(\beta\in(0,1)\).
  • The sequence problem: \(V^*(x_0)=\max_{\{x_{t+1}\}}\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tF(x_t,x_{t+1})\) s.t. \(x_{t+1}\in\Gamma(x_t)\), with \(x_0\) given.
  • Control/state form \([X,U,h,g,\beta]\): \(\max\sum\beta^th(x_t,u_t)\) s.t. \(x_{t+1}=g(x_t,u_t)\), \(u_t\in U\).
  • Map to \(F,\Gamma\): \(F(x,y)=\max_u\{h(x,u):u\in U,\ y=g(x,u)\}\), and \(\Gamma(x)=\{y:\exists u\in U,\ y=g(x,u)\}\).
  • States are predetermined stocks (\(k\), \(h\), assets). Controls are chosen flows (\(c\), \(i\), \(n\), \(\ell\)). The \(F\)-form eliminates the controls.

Euler equation and transversality L27 · slide 11

Assume \(X\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), \(F\in C^1\). A path satisfies the EE if \(F_y(x_t,x_{t+1})+\beta F_x(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})=0\) for all \(t\ge0\), and the TC if \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta^tF_x(x_t,x_{t+1})\cdot x_t=0\).

NGM: \(U'(c_t)=\beta U'(c_{t+1})f'(k_{t+1})\) and \(\lim\beta^tU'(c_t)f'(k_t)k_t=0\).

  • EE: the marginal cost of raising \(x_{t+1}\) today (\(-F_y\)) equals its discounted marginal return tomorrow (\(\beta F_x\)). This is a one-period perturbation that leaves the rest of the path unchanged.
  • TC: the discounted shadow value of the stock held "at infinity" is zero, so there is no over-accumulation. It is the boundary condition that pins down \(x_1\) given \(x_0\).
  • "Under appropriate concavity, interiority and regularity conditions, EE + TC are necessary and sufficient." The 2026 slide 11 adds the qualifier; the 2025 deck stated it unconditionally.

Sufficiency theorem L27 · slides 12–17 · SLP Thm 4.15

Assume (1) \(F\) concave in \((x,y)\), (2) \(F_x(x^*_t,x^*_{t+1})\ge0\) along the candidate path, and (3) \(X=\mathbb R^m_+\). Then a path \(\{x^*_{t+1}\}\) satisfying the EE and TC is optimal.

  1. Concavity: \(F(x,y)\le F(x^*,y^*)+F_x\cdot(x-x^*)+F_y\cdot(y-y^*)\), for any feasible \(\{x_t\}\) with \(x_0=x^*_0\).
  2. Sum \(\beta^t\times\) the inequality over \(t\le T\), and drop the \(t=0\) term \(F_x(x_0-x_0^*)=0\).
  3. Regroup by \((x_{t+1}-x^*_{t+1})\). Each coefficient is \(\beta^t[F_y+\beta F_x]=0\) by the EE, leaving \(\beta^TF_y(x^*_T,x^*_{T+1})(x_{T+1}-x^*_{T+1})\).
  4. Use the EE once more: this equals \(-\beta^{T+1}F_x(x^*_{T+1},x^*_{T+2})(x_{T+1}-x^*_{T+1})\le\beta^{T+1}F_x\,x^*_{T+1}\), because \(x_{T+1}\ge0\) (hypothesis 3) and \(F_x\ge0\) (hypothesis 2).
  5. TC ⇒ the bound → 0, so \(\lim_T\sum\beta^t[F(x_t,x_{t+1})-F(x^*_t,x^*_{t+1})]\le0\).
  • Concave \(f\): \(f(x)\le f(x^0)+f'(x^0)(x-x^0)\); the graph lies below every tangent (slide 13). With \(C^2\), the second-order Taylor term \(\frac12(x-x^0)'D^2f(x-x^0)\le0\).
  • \(X=\mathbb R_+\) fails when \(\Gamma=\mathbb R\), as in the quadratic adjustment-cost model. Then argue directly: use the quadratic value function, or restrict to paths with \(\beta^tx_t^2\to0\).

Necessity and uniqueness L28 · slides 19–25

  • Variations: \(x_t(\alpha)=x_t+\alpha\varepsilon_t\) with \(\varepsilon_0=0\), because \(x_0\) is given. Optimality ⇒ \(v'(0)=0\), provided \(v\) is differentiable and the limits in \(\alpha\) and \(T\) can be interchanged.
  • EE necessary given interiority. There must be a neighbourhood of \(x_{t+1}\) in which every \(z\) has \(z\in\Gamma(x_t)\) and \(x_{t+2}\in\Gamma(z)\), with \(x_{t+1}\in\operatorname{Int}X\). Both adjacent constraints must be slack; the 2026 deck states both, while 2025 stated only one.
  • The simplest proof perturbs one coordinate: \(\max_z F(x_t,z)+\beta F(z,x_{t+2})\). It needs no value function and no envelope condition.
  • TC necessary (heuristic): use the EE to get \(v'(0)=-\lim\beta^{T+1}F_x\,\varepsilon_{T+1}\), then take \(\varepsilon_{T+1}=-x_{T+1}\), which scales the stock to 0. The 2026 slide 23 flags this as heuristic, because the variation changes with \(T\). It uses no concavity.
  • EE as a second-order difference equation: \(x_{t+2}=\psi(x_{t+1},x_t)\). It is well defined (by the IFT) if \(F\in C^2\) and \(F_{xy}(x_{t+1},x_{t+2})\ne0\), because \(\partial[\text{EE}]/\partial x_{t+2}=\beta F_{xy}\). There is one initial condition (\(x_0\)) and one terminal condition (the TC).
  • At most one solution if \(X\) is convex, \(\Gamma\) has a convex graph, and \(F\) is strictly concave. The proof: average two optima, which is feasible and strictly better.
  • Shooting: given \(x_0\), guess \(x_1\), iterate \(\psi\), and check the TC. Adjust \(x_1\) by bisection: too little saving makes \(k\) hit 0, and too much makes \(k\) head to \(k_{\max}\) with \(c\to0\), which violates the TC. It works for one-dimensional saddle-path problems, where paths are monotone in \(x_1\) and exactly one converges to \(\bar x\). It is numerically unstable, because errors explode along the unstable root.

Steady states L27 · slide 18

  • A steady state satisfies \(F_y(\bar x,\bar x)+\beta F_x(\bar x,\bar x)=0\). Staying at \(\bar x\) from \(x_0=\bar x\) is optimal when the sufficiency theorem applies. The TC is automatic: \(\beta^tF_x(\bar x,\bar x)\bar x\to0\).
  • NGM: \(\beta f'(\bar k)=1\iff G_k(\bar k)=\frac1\beta-1+\delta=\rho+\delta\). Under Inada it is unique, and it rises with \(\beta\). This is the modified golden rule. The golden rule \(\max_k f(k)-k\) gives \(f'(k_{gr})=1\) (\(G_k=\delta\)), so \(\bar k\lt k_{gr}\): impatience. Compare Diamond and BY, where the CE can sit above the golden rule (L15, L19).
  • Invariance: the steady state is unchanged by \(U\circ F\) (factor \(U'(F(\bar x,\bar x))\)), by \(-\frac b2(y-x)^2\) (zero on the diagonal), and by the form of \(u\) in the NGM.
  • Savings with \(\beta(1+r)=1\): every \(x_0>-w/r\) is a steady state, a continuum. Cake eating \(F=\ln(x-y)\): no interior steady state.

Reading the investment EE

\(a'(\bar g)=\beta\cdot\frac{A-a(\bar g)}{1-\beta\bar g}\). The marginal cost of growing one more unit equals the discounted value of one more unit of installed capital. Here \(A-a(\bar g)\) is the dividend per unit of capital, and \(\frac1{1-\beta\bar g}=\sum_t(\beta\bar g)^t\) capitalizes a dividend that grows at \(\bar g\). Equivalently, \(A=a(1+\gamma)+a'(1+\gamma)(r-\gamma)\).

Which q, and which cost ordering?

The slide 52 \(q=V(k)/k\) is cum-dividend, so zero growth gives \(q=1/\beta\), not 1. The ex-dividend value per unit of next-period capital is \(\beta V(1)=a'(\bar g)\); the 2025 deck's "q = \(a'\)" refers to that object. A pointwise higher cost \(\tilde a>a\) does not by itself lower \(\bar g\), because the EE involves \(a'\). A sufficient condition is \(\beta\tilde a+\tilde a'(1-\beta g)>\beta a+a'(1-\beta g)\) on \((1,1/\beta)\), as the 2026 slide 49 now says.

Notation clashes in folder 4
  • \(F\) is the period return, not a production function. \(G(k,n)\) is production, and \(f(k)=G(k,1)+(1-\delta)k\) includes undepreciated capital, so \(\beta f'(\bar k)=1\) (not \(f'=\rho+\delta\)).
  • \(\alpha\): the Cobb–Douglas capital share (slide 35) or the CES weight (slide 37). Earlier it was the old-age endowment (Note II) or the income-decline rate (BY). In slide 19, \(\alpha\) is the size of a variation.
  • \(\gamma\): the policy root (slide 34), then the CRRA curvature or homogeneity degree \(1-\gamma\) (slides 41–44), then the net growth rate \(\gamma=\bar g-1\) (slide 50).
  • \(a,b\): cost weights (slide 32); \(a(\cdot)\): the adjustment-cost function (slide 47). \(\rho\): the CES elasticity (slide 37); \(\sigma\): the IES. \(r\): the net interest rate with \(\beta=1/(1+r)\).
  • \(g\): the policy function \(y=g(x)\) or the control-state law of motion \(g(x,u)\). \(\bar g\): the gross growth factor.
  • \(\beta\) is the discount factor here; in Note II it was a preference weight.
