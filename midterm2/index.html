Midterm 2: Four Sessions, Four to Five Hours

Covers Aggregation (Lessons 21–25), Euler equations & transversality (Lessons 26–32), Local stability & linearization (Lessons 33–35), and the Hamiltonian (Lessons 36–39) · Course index
How this guide works. Each session is one or two long problems drawn directly from past midterms and finals, reformatted for self-study. Work on paper as if it were the real exam — number your answers, enforce the line limits, and don't look at the solution until you're done. Then reveal and self-grade against the rubric. Misses join your review queue below. Session 4 is a timed mock (90 min).
  • Aggregation (Session 1) is the most algebraically intensive and has the richest past-exam record. Do not skip the derivation sub-parts even if the conclusion seems obvious.
  • Euler equations & transversality (Session 2) connects the discrete-time tools in Folder 5 to the continuous-time Hamiltonian. The speed-of-convergence problem has appeared on MT1 in past years, so it is fair game.
  • Linearization (Session 3) is short but technically precise — eigenvalue signs and the Hamiltonian matrix are reliable exam territory.
  • Hamiltonian (Session 4 and the mock) is the heaviest folder and appeared on both the Fall 2025 midterm 2 and many prior finals. Budget the most time there.

5. Common errors to avoid

  • Sharing rule confusion. The planner's sharing rule \(\delta^i\) depends on \(\lambda_i\) and \(\sigma\) but is the same for every good \(l\). Many students re-derive it for each good — do it once and invoke the result. L23 §3
  • Getting the Hamiltonian matrix ordering wrong. With \(\hat z = (\hat\lambda, \hat k)'\), the matrix is \(\mathcal H = \begin{pmatrix}\rho - H^*_{k\lambda} & -H^*_{kk} \\ H^*_{\lambda\lambda} & H^*_{\lambda k}\end{pmatrix}\). With \(\hat z = (\hat k, \hat\lambda)'\) the blocks swap. Many past exams (and solutions floating around) use the wrong ordering. L38 §3
  • Forgetting transversality. In a Hamiltonian problem, writing the FOC and costate is not enough — you need TC \(\lim_{T\to\infty} e^{-\rho T}\lambda(T)k(T) = 0\). It is what pins the initial shadow price. L36 §3
  • Using the wrong sign for the costate equation. The costate equation is \(\dot\lambda = \rho\lambda - \partial\mathcal H/\partial k\), not \(\dot\lambda = -\partial\mathcal H/\partial k\). The \(\rho\lambda\) term accounts for the discount rate. L36 §3
  • Permanent government shock changes \(k^*\). It does not. The \(\dot c = 0\) locus is \(f'(k^*) = \rho\), which is independent of \(g\). Only the \(\dot k = 0\) locus (and hence \(c^*\)) shifts. L37 §3
  • Speed of convergence \(g'(\bar x)\) is \(\lambda_1\), not \(b\). It is the smaller eigenvalue of the quadratic \(Q(\lambda) = 1 + b\lambda + \beta\lambda^2\), not \(b\) itself. And \(\lambda_1\) is increasing in \(b\), so a less negative \(b\) means slower convergence. L33 §2
