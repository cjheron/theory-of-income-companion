Part I. True or false? Justify in 1–4 lines (120 points)

20 pts
"In an exchange economy with finitely many goods, if one consumer's preferences violate local non-satiation, the competitive equilibrium allocation is not Pareto optimal."

False. LNS is sufficient for the 1WT, not necessary. Without it the conclusion can fail (a satiated consumer may hold goods it does not value at the margin), but it need not. Example: a consumer with a unique bliss point who consumes exactly it in the CE. Any allocation it weakly prefers gives it the same bundle, so the 1WT argument goes through for the others, and the CE is PO.

20: "False; LNS is sufficient, not necessary" + an example or the reason. 10: right call, reason only "the theorem doesn't apply".
20 pts
"In the Note II OLG economy with \(v=(1-\beta)\log c_y+\beta\log c_o\), endowments \((1-\alpha,\alpha)\) and \(\beta>\alpha\), some PAYG taxes \(\tau>\beta-\alpha\) are still Pareto improvements over the competitive equilibrium."

True. With PAYG the CE is still no trade at the after-tax endowments, so each generation \(t\ge1\) gets \(W(\tau)=v(1-\alpha-\tau,\alpha+\tau)\). \(W\) is strictly concave, maximized at \(\tau=\beta-\alpha\), and continuous, so \(W(\tau)>W(0)\) for all \(\tau\in(0,\bar\tau)\) with \(\bar\tau>\beta-\alpha\). The initial old gain \(\tau\) for any \(\tau>0\).

20: concavity of \(W\), max at \(\beta-\alpha\), continuity ⇒ an interval beyond it, initial old gain. 10: right call, "a little over the optimum still beats autarky" without the argument.
20 pts
"In the Diamond model with population growth \(n>0\), a steady state with a strictly positive interest rate can be dynamically inefficient."

True. With population growth, the golden rule is \(F_K-\delta=n\), not \(0\). If \(0<\bar r\lt n\), capital per worker is above the golden rule: lowering it permanently raises consumption per capita at every date, so the CE is Pareto-dominated. Equivalently, the aggregate endowment grows at \(1+n\) while prices fall at \(1+\bar r<1+n\), so its value is infinite. "\(r>0\) ⇒ efficient" holds only without growth.

20: the criterion \(\bar r\) vs \(n\) (or the infinite-value argument). 10: right call, no criterion.
20 pts
"In Blanchard–Yaari with capital and \(\alpha=0\), a permanently higher level of government debt, with lump-sum taxes adjusted every instant to pay its interest, lowers the steady-state capital stock. The effect vanishes as \(p\to0\)."

True. With \(T=rB+G\), the steady state solves \([\mathcal F'(K)-\theta](\mathcal F(K)-G)=p(p+\theta)(B+K)\). A higher \(B\) raises the right side, so \(K^*\) falls and \(r^*\) rises: debt is net wealth to people alive today, because part of the taxes falls on the unborn. As \(p\to0\) the right side vanishes and \(\mathcal F'(K^*)=\theta\) regardless of \(B\): Ricardian equivalence.

20: the steady-state condition (or the "debt is net wealth since \(p>0\)" logic) and the \(p\to0\) limit. 10: one of the two.
20 pts
"Pure exchange economy with differentiable, strictly increasing, concave utilities. Suppose a CE allocation is interior, so it solves the planner's problem for some weights. Then those weights are, up to scale, the inverses \(1/\mu_i\) of the households' marginal utilities of income, and no other weights work."

True. At an interior allocation, any supporting weights satisfy the planner's FOC \(\lambda_iu^i_l=\gamma_l\), and the CE gives \(u^i_l=\mu_ip_l\). So \(\lambda_i\mu_ip_l=\gamma_l\) for all \(i\) and \(l\). Fixing \(l\) with \(p_l>0\), \(\lambda_i\mu_i\) is the same for every \(i\): \(\lambda_i=\kappa/\mu_i\), with \(\gamma=\kappa p\).

20: both FOCs and the "\(\lambda_i\mu_i\) is common across \(i\)" step. 10: states \(\lambda_i=1/\mu_i\) without showing uniqueness.
20 pts
"In the OLG endowment economy with log utility, endowments \((1-\alpha,\alpha)\) and population growth \(n\), if \(\bar r>n\), introducing PAYG social security (each young pays \(\tau>0\), each old receives \((1+n)\tau\)) makes every generation born after its introduction worse off."

True. The CE stays no trade at the after-tax endowments, so a generation born after the reform gets \(W(\tau)=v(1-\alpha-\tau,\alpha+(1+n)\tau)\). Then \(W'(0)=-v_1+(1+n)v_2=v_2[(1+n)-(1+\bar r)]<0\), using \(v_1/v_2=1+\bar r\) at the endowment. \(W\) is concave, so \(W(\tau)\lt W(0)\) for every \(\tau>0\). Only the initial old gain. PAYG returns \(1+n\) while the market returns \(1+\bar r\).

20: \(W'(0)\) sign via MRS \(=1+\bar r\), plus concavity for all \(\tau\). 10: "PAYG returns n < r" without the derivative.

Part II. OLG with population growth and government debt (70 points)

Time \(t=1,2,\dots\). Generation \(t\) has \(N_t=(1+n)^t\) members, \(n>0\), each with utility \((1-\beta)\log c^t_t+\beta\log c^t_{t+1}\) and endowments \(1-\alpha\) young and \(\alpha\) old. The \(N_0=1\) initial old have utility \(c^0_1\) and endowment \(\alpha\) each. One good per date. Each answer: at most 2 lines unless stated.

40 pts

No government.

  1. (10 pts, 1 line) Write goods-market clearing at date \(t\).
  2. (10 pts) Argue that the equilibrium is no trade, and give \(\bar r\). Does it depend on \(n\)?
  3. (10 pts) Compute the value of the aggregate endowment at CE prices (\(p_1=1\)). When is it finite?
  4. (10 pts) For which parameters does the 1WT guarantee the CE is PO? For which is it Pareto-dominated by a symmetric allocation? (No proof needed for the second.)

1. \(N_tc^t_t+N_{t-1}c^{t-1}_t=N_t(1-\alpha)+N_{t-1}\alpha\), i.e. \((1+n)c^t_t+c^{t-1}_t=(1+n)(1-\alpha)+\alpha\) per old person.
2. The initial old can only consume their endowment (they value only date 1 and have nobody to trade with who would repay them). Then date-1 clearing gives the young \(1-\alpha\), their budget gives \(\alpha\) when old, and so on by induction. \(\bar r\) is the MRS at the endowment: \(\bar r=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\beta(1-\alpha)}\), independent of \(n\).
3. Aggregate endowment at \(t\) is \(N_t(1-\alpha)+N_{t-1}\alpha=(1+n)^{t-1}[(1+n)(1-\alpha)+\alpha]\), and \(p_t=(1+\bar r)^{-(t-1)}\). The value is \([(1+n)(1-\alpha)+\alpha]\sum_{t\ge1}\big(\frac{1+n}{1+\bar r}\big)^{t-1}\), finite iff \(1+\bar r>1+n\).
4. 1WT: PO if \(\bar r>n\). Pareto-dominated by the best symmetric allocation if \(\bar r\lt n\).

Part 2 needs the initial-old induction; "autarky satisfies the FOCs" only shows it is an equilibrium (half credit).
30 pts

Rolled-over debt. At date 1 the government sells \(b\) units of one-period debt to each young person, gives the proceeds to the initial old, and from then on repays maturing debt (with interest) only by selling new debt \(b\) to each new young person. No taxes. Assume \(\bar r\lt n\).

  1. (10 pts) Write the government's budget constraint at \(t\ge2\) and show that a stationary equilibrium with \(b>0\) has \(1+r=1+n\).
  2. (10 pts) Find \(b\), and show \(b>0\) iff \(\bar r\lt n\).
  3. (10 pts, 3 lines) Show every generation \(t\ge1\) consumes the best symmetric allocation, and that the policy Pareto-improves on the CE.

5. \(N_tb=(1+r_{t-1})N_{t-1}b\) (new debt sold = old debt repaid with interest), so \(1+r=N_t/N_{t-1}=1+n\).
6. Asset clearing: each young person's saving equals \(b\), so \(b=s(n)=\beta(1-\alpha)-\frac{(1-\beta)\alpha}{1+n}\). This is positive iff \(1+n>\frac{(1-\beta)\alpha}{\beta(1-\alpha)}=1+\bar r\).
7. Consumption: \(c_y=1-\alpha-b=(1-\beta)\big[(1-\alpha)+\frac{\alpha}{1+n}\big]\) and \(c_o=\alpha+(1+n)b=\beta[(1+n)(1-\alpha)+\alpha]\). That is the maximizer of \(v\) on the normalized resource line \((1+n)c_y+c_o=(1+n)(1-\alpha)+\alpha\), the best symmetric allocation, identical to optimal PAYG. Each generation strictly prefers it to its endowment (which is on the same line), and the initial old get \(\alpha+(1+n)b>\alpha\). Pareto improvement.

Part III. Planner, beliefs and Negishi weights (60 points)

One good, two states \(s=1,2\), two agents \(i=1,2\). Agent \(i\) has subjective probabilities \(\pi^i_s>0\) (\(\pi^i_1+\pi^i_2=1\)) and utility \(u^i(x^i)=\sum_s\pi^i_s\log x^i_s\). Aggregate endowment \(\bar e_s>0\). Complete markets for state-contingent goods.

30 pts
  1. (10 pts, 1 line) The planner's FOC for \(x^i_s\), with weights \(\lambda_i>0\) and multipliers \(\gamma_s\).
  2. (10 pts, 2 lines) Solve for \(\gamma_s\) and \(x^i_s\) as functions of \(\lambda\).
  3. (10 pts, 3 lines) With common beliefs, show \(x^i_s\) depends on \(s\) only through \(\bar e_s\). With different beliefs and \(\bar e_1=\bar e_2\), show agent 1 consumes more in state 1 than in state 2 iff \(\pi^1_1/\pi^2_1>\pi^1_2/\pi^2_2\).

1. \(\lambda_i\pi^i_s/x^i_s=\gamma_s\).
2. \(x^i_s=\lambda_i\pi^i_s/\gamma_s\). Feasibility: \(\gamma_s=(\lambda_1\pi^1_s+\lambda_2\pi^2_s)/\bar e_s\). So \(x^i_s=\dfrac{\lambda_i\pi^i_s}{\lambda_1\pi^1_s+\lambda_2\pi^2_s}\,\bar e_s\).
3. Common \(\pi\): the share is \(\lambda_i/(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)\), constant, so \(x^i_s\) depends on \(s\) only through \(\bar e_s\). With \(\bar e_1=\bar e_2\), agent 1's share in state \(s\) is \(\big(1+\frac{\lambda_2\pi^2_s}{\lambda_1\pi^1_s}\big)^{-1}\), larger when \(\pi^1_s/\pi^2_s\) is larger. So \(x^1_1>x^1_2\) iff \(\pi^1_1/\pi^2_1>\pi^1_2/\pi^2_2\) (equivalently \(\pi^1_1>\pi^2_1\)). Agents bet.

30 pts
  1. (10 pts, 2 lines) Show \(\gamma\cdot x^i(\lambda)=\lambda_i\), and write the condition on \(\lambda\) under which the planner's allocation, with \(p=\gamma\), is a CE for endowments \(e^i\).
  2. (10 pts, 3 lines) Let \(\bar e_1=\bar e_2=1\), \(e^1=(1,0)\), \(e^2=(0,1)\), and normalize \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1\). Solve for the Negishi weight \(\lambda_1\). Check your answer when beliefs are common.
  3. (10 pts, 2 lines) Write \(p_1/p_2\). Does it depend on the distribution of endowments? Contrast with common beliefs.

4. \(\gamma\cdot x^i=\sum_s\gamma_s\lambda_i\pi^i_s/\gamma_s=\lambda_i\sum_s\pi^i_s=\lambda_i\). CE condition (budgets balance): \(\lambda_i=\gamma(\lambda)\cdot e^i\) for each \(i\) (one is redundant by Walras).
5. \(\lambda_1=\gamma_1=\lambda_1\pi^1_1+(1-\lambda_1)\pi^2_1\), so \(\lambda_1(1-\pi^1_1+\pi^2_1)=\pi^2_1\) and \(\lambda_1=\dfrac{\pi^2_1}{\pi^1_2+\pi^2_1}\). Common beliefs: \(\lambda_1=\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_2+\pi_1}=\pi_1\), the value of a claim to one unit in state 1. ✓
6. \(\dfrac{p_1}{p_2}=\dfrac{\gamma_1}{\gamma_2}=\dfrac{\lambda_1\pi^1_1+\lambda_2\pi^2_1}{\lambda_1\pi^1_2+\lambda_2\pi^2_2}\cdot\dfrac{\bar e_2}{\bar e_1}\). With different beliefs it depends on the weights, hence on the distribution of endowments: more wealth to the agent who thinks state 1 is likely raises \(p_1/p_2\). With common beliefs it is \(\frac{\pi_1\bar e_2}{\pi_2\bar e_1}\), independent of the distribution.
